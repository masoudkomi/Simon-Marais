2026-10-16 UTC. This supersedes the abstract-only Chen-Mihailescu lead in
the frozen initial references and literature-audit notes. Both reviewers
independently checked the primary theorem statements and relevant context;
they did not re-prove the complete published papers.

Chen and Mihailescu, *New Conditions on the Nagell-Ljunggren Equation*,
Chinese Annals of Mathematics B 47 (2026), 389-412,
[DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11401-026-0018-7),
[complete public journal PDF](https://camath.fudan.edu.cn/cambcn/ch/reader/create_pdf.aspx?file_no=47B208&flag=1).
Theorem 1.2 concerns the nontrivial integer domain |x|,|y|>1, prime
term count p>=5, an odd prime q distinct from p, and

    (x^p-1)/(x-1) = p^e*y^q,
    e=1 when x=1 mod p, otherwise e=0.

Its condition is q not dividing the minus class number h_p^- of
Q(zeta_p). The printed conclusion expressly retains possible exceptions
when **p<29 and x<0**. Its footnote explains the positive-base origin
of the bound 29. The abstract omits this exception. The diagonal p=q,
class-number-divisible cases, composite term counts and boundary values
cannot be silently included. The proof's complete chain is not validated
by a theorem-statement audit. These restrictions do not give a full
solution to the contest's integer-domain problem.

Both reviewers also confirmed the interpretation of Bennett-Levin's
2015 Theorem 1 as a **necessary** bound Omega(m)<=3, not a solution
through that range, and confirmed that Bugeaud-Mihailescu's 2007
29 bound has a positive-base hypothesis. The positive-domain reduction
to prime term counts is a sufficiency reduction for proving nonexistence,
not an assertion that every original solution has prime term count.

The independently confirmed Son 2026 objections concern its literal
all-integer statement (which misses (-1,1)) and an incorrect rho
coefficient used in the printed argument. They do not refute a repaired
nontrivial theorem. Both reviewers independently confirmed Wang v1's
missing factor T in equation (33), and that the later bound depends on
that smaller expression. This is an objection to the supplied argument,
not a counterexample to the conjectural conclusion or to abc.
