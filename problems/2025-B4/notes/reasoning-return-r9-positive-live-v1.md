The exact fixed cover remains W^p=Phi_p(T), U^p=P_theta(T). At its infinite
point Q_b one has T=s^-p and U/W tending to zeta^b. Thus U-zeta^c W has
a pole of order p-1 when b!=c. At b=c its leading terms cancel; it has
order at least1, and exactly1 when the first coefficient of the two
canonical root series differs. The latter coefficient cannot vanish
for an original theta!=N: equality would say sum n_a zeta^a=-1, so the
unique Phi_p relation would force all n_a=1. The resulting product
prod_(c!=b0)(U-zeta^c W) therefore has pole order(p-1)^2 at Q_b0 but
still has pole order(p-2)(p-1)-1 at every other infinite point. For p>=37
that order is positive. This simple product does NOT give the zero-safe
single-pole family required by the frozen Runge argument.

Dividing by P_theta-Phi_p merely rewrites such a quotient as a reciprocal
of U-zeta^c W. Its finite zeros can then produce finite poles. No proof
that such poles cancel in a useful integral auxiliary function was found.
It would be false to announce an integral-function replacement without
checking the complete finite divisor.

a SET of unused infinite branches. Complex-conjugate embeddings may
prohibit the same branch after the actual real descent is carefully
proved. The needed branch pairing, resulting divisor, coefficient heights
and comparison with the published diagonal heights have NOT been
established as a new theorem. In particular this observation gives no
numerical improvement or arithmetic exclusion.
