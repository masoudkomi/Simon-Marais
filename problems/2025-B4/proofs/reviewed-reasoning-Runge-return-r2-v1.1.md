## 3. The character/L-value normalization and precise analytic import

We first derive the exact normalization rather than import a functional
equation with a possibly wrong conjugate or factor p. Let psi be ANY
nontrivial odd character mod the prime p, extended by 0 on multiples
of p. It is primitive of conductor p. Define

    tau(psi)=sum_(a=1)^(p-1) psi(a) zeta_p^a,
    B_(1,psi)=(1/p)sum_(a=1)^(p-1) a psi(a).

The finite Gauss identity is

    sum_a psi(a) zeta_p^(an)=bar(psi)(n)*tau(psi)

for every integer n, including p|n when both sides vanish. For p not
dividing n it is the substitution a->a/n. Moreover |tau(psi)|^2=p:
write a=tb in the squared sum; the inner sum over b is p-1 if t=1
and -1 otherwise, and sum_t psi(t)=0.

For 0<rho<1 absolute convergence of the logarithm series now gives

    sum_(n>=1) bar(psi)(n) rho^n/n
      =-tau(psi)^(-1) sum_a psi(a) log(1-rho*zeta_p^a).

The periodic coefficients bar(psi)(n) have bounded partial sums and
mean0. Summation by parts bounds their tail with weights rho^n/n by
a constant/N, uniformly for 0<rho<=1. Hence the left side tends to
L(1,bar(psi)) as rho tends to1. On the right the logarithm continued
from rho0 is the principal one and is continuous through rho1: none
of these segments hits a zero or the negative real axis. Precisely,

    log(1-zeta_p^a)=log(2*sin(pi*a/p))
                       + i*pi*(a/p-1/2).

Pairing a with p-a cancels the real term for odd psi. The constant
imaginary term cancels because sum psi=0. Therefore

    L(1,bar(psi))=-i*pi*B_(1,psi)/tau(psi),
    |B_(1,psi)|=sqrt(p)/pi * |L(1,bar(psi))|.       (15)
