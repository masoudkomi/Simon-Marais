## 1. RG1: geometric integrality and the actual K-point

The vectors (1)_(a in G) and(n_a) in F_p^(p-1) are independent. If
a(1)+b(n)=0 and b!=0, then n is constant modulo p. Since its coefficient
sum is p-1=-1 modulo p, the constant must be1. The canonical coefficients

If b=0 then a=0. This also proves independence over the algebraic closure
in the following Kummer test: a product Phi^a P_theta^b can be a p-th
power in overline(K)(T) only if every valuation at T=zeta^c is a multiple
of p, which is exactly the impossible nonzero vector relation above.

Consequently(3) has geometrically integral function field

    L=K(T)(W,U), [L:K(T)]=p^2, Gal(L/K(T))=F_p^2.     (4)

Here the usual elementary Kummer degree criterion is applied over a
field of characteristic0 containing all p-th roots of unity. One may
prove it by adjoining W first: a failure of the second degree p would
give a p-th-power relation between its two radicands, contradicting the
valuation test. The same test holds over overline(K), so no extra
constant field or geometrically split component is being ignored.

For an actual solution(0), (T,W,U)=(X,Y,beta) is a K-point of(3).
Since X is not any zeta^a and Y,beta are nonzero, the derivatives of the
two p-th-power equations in W,U are nonzero. This point is smooth and
lies off the branch locus; it has a unique K-rational lift P to C. Thus

## 2. RG2: a finite family that cannot all evaluate to zero

    D_c=d Q_c-sum_(b!=c) Q_b.                       (9)

f_(c,1),...,f_(c,g+1) of its Riemann--Roch space L(D_c). Riemann--Roch

regular at all finite points, has its only possible pole at Q_c of

For EVERY finite point P in C(K), at least one f_(c,l)(P) is nonzero.
Indeed D_c-P has degree2g-1>2g-2, so

    dim L(D_c)-dim L(D_c-P)=1.                       (10)

At a finite P the coefficient of D_c is0, and membership in L(D_c-P)

zero. No assertion that an arbitrarily selected one is nonzero is made.

Each f_(c,l) is integral over K[T]. To see this, the normalization of

## 3. A finite exact construction of the required spaces

This section makes the geometric data effective in this PARTICULAR
model. It is an algorithmic specification, not a run or numerical table.
For 0<=i,j<p set

    B_(i,j)=U^i W^j /
       prod_a(T-zeta^a)^floor((i n_a+j)/p),
    k_(i,j)=(i+j)(p-1)-p sum_a floor((i n_a+j)/p)
           =sum_a ((i n_a+j) mod p).                 (12)

Then k_(i,j)>=0. These B_(i,j) form a K[T]-basis of the integral closure
of K[T] in L. A proof avoids assuming a favorable affine presentation.
The p^2 Kummer characters split L into its one-dimensional K(T)-eigenlines
K(T) U^i W^j. Character projections preserve integrality over K[T], since
their denominators p^2 are units in K[T]. On one eigenline a rational
coefficient can have finite poles only at T=zeta^a. At that place,
ord_Q(T-zeta^a)=p and ord_Q(U^i W^j)=i n_a+j. The least correction
required for nonnegative valuation is exactly the floor in(12). At every
other finite place U,W are units. This proves both spanning and
independence of the integral basis, with no factorization of a solution's
Y or its characteristic ideal.

At each Q_c, B_(i,j) has pole order k_(i,j) and leading coefficient
zeta^(ci), in the parameter(6). A function with poles of order at most d
at EVERY Q_c is an exact K-linear combination of

    T^h B_(i,j), h>=0, p h+k_(i,j)<=d.              (13)

To justify the upper degree bound, suppose its polynomial-basis
representation had a largest pole number H>d. All terms with that H
have the same i+j modulo p, since k_(i,j)=-(i+j) modulo p. For a fixed i
there is then at most one possible j in0,...,p-1 and one h at that H.
Their leading values at Q_c form a linear combination of the characters
zeta^(ci), c in F_p. If they canceled at ALL p infinite points, the
invertible p by p Fourier matrix would make every coefficient zero,
contradicting the definition of H. Thus no unbounded hidden cancellation
is being used to truncate a Riemann--Roch search.

Start with the finite span(13), and impose, using the series(6), the
linear vanishing of all Laurent coefficients with exponents -d,...,0
at each Q_b with b!=c. Its solution space is EXACTLY L(D_c): it enforces
regularity and a zero of order>=1 there. The pole bound at Q_c already
holds by(13). Exact linear algebra over K finds a basis. Rescale via
the explicit monic Galois equations as in section2. These operations
depend only on p and the chosen theta, not on a solution or its phases.
They may be large; no efficiency or actual computation is claimed.

Write each resulting scaled function in its basis form

    F_(c,l)=sum_(i,j,h) A_(c,l,i,j,h) T^h B_(i,j),
    A_(...)=sum_(r=0)^(p-2) a_(...,r) zeta^r,
    a_(...,r) in Q.                                 (14)

All these are finite lists of exact coefficients. Define the explicit
positive rational constant

    M=2 max_(c,l) sum_(i,j,h,r)
                     |a_(c,l,i,j,h,r)| 2^(p(h+i+j)). (15)

A nonzero RR basis makes M>0. Formula(15) is a finite certificate formula;
no uncomputed coefficient is silently replaced by a claimed numerical
constant. The rational a-values and the monic-integrality equations can
be checked independently if an actual numerical certificate is produced.

    |U|,|W|<=2^p, |T-zeta^a|>=2^p-1>=1.            (16)
