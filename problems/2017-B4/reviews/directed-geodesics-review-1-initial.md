## Background actually assumed

Let \(D\) be fixed, \(v_i=(1,i-1)\), \(V=(v_1,\ldots,v_D)\), and
\[
Z=\sum_i[0,m_iv_i].
\]
For the disk construction, \(m_i\) are nonnegative integers and the active
directions span the plane. An edge-to-edge tiling uses the nondegenerate
parallelograms generated by pairs of these directions. There are exactly \(m_i\)
color-\(i\) de Bruijn tracks; different-color tracks cross once and same-color
tracks never cross. The canonical lift satisfies \(Va(x)=x\), and a positive
color-\(i\) edge changes the lift by \(e_i\). I use the conventional normalization
\(a(0)=0\) at the marked initial vertex.

These are the candidate's explicit background premises. I have not treated
unreviewed statements of the other fixedD packet as additional accepted
dependencies. In particular, the proof does not use profile mixing, boundary
padding, weak-order envelopes, local-pattern entropy, or an annulus extension.
The normalized lift can be constructed by integrating the edge labels: the
increment sum is zero around every tile, and a tiled disk has no nontrivial
homology obstruction. This observation explains the normalization; it is not a
new assumption about profiles.

## Primary theorem check

I read Section 2 and the complete proof of Lemma 2.2 in the original
Arroyo--McQuillan--Richter--Salazar author preprint, dated 2015-11-20, printed
pp. 4--5. Their arrangement is finite, its arcs separate the plane, and two arcs
may be disjoint; otherwise their sole intersection must be a crossing. The
lemma gives a dual path between off-arc points meeting each arc at most once.
The proof inducts after crossing an incident separating arc and restricting the
remaining arrangement to its destination side.

Our extended tracks are finite in number, piecewise linear, proper and simple,
with only ordinary pairwise crossings. They satisfy these hypotheses. The
projective enlargement theorem in the same section is not the theorem used:
requiring every pair to cross would be inappropriate for repeated colors.

## DG1, step 1: the interior cell complex

In a tile \(b+[0,v_i]+[0,v_j]\), join the midpoints of opposite color-\(i\)
edges and similarly for color \(j\). The two segments cross transversely at the
tile center. There are four open sectors, naturally attached to the four tile
vertices. Along a shared tile edge its midpoint is the only track point; the
two half-edges and their neighborhoods glue sectors with the same endpoint.
Thus moving through the complement without crossing a track cannot change the
vertex label.

Conversely, all sectors bearing one vertex label connect through a sufficiently
small neighborhood of that vertex, because no track passes through a tiling
vertex. This includes a vertex of arbitrary valence. The edge-to-edge
assumption excludes a T-junction or an extra vertex in the interior of an edge.
Hence the components of the track complement inside the disk are exactly
connected vertex stars, including truncated stars at boundary vertices.

Between consecutive tile-center crossings along a track there is exactly one
tiling edge of its color. A segment from the last crossing to a boundary end
likewise corresponds to one boundary edge. Its two incident complement
components have the two endpoints of that edge as their labels. These
observations establish the intended adjacency, before considering the exterior.

## DG1, step 2: the radial extension and the absence of shortcuts

Choose a point \(o\) in the interior of the two-dimensional convex zonogon.
For an endpoint \(p\) of a track on the relative interior of a boundary unit
edge, attach the ray
\[
\{o+t(p-o):t\geq1\}.
\]
Convexity and interiority imply that the ray beyond \(p\) is outside \(Z\).
Every ray from \(o\) has exactly one boundary intersection, so distinct
endpoints have distinct directions. The exterior rays are pairwise disjoint,
and none meets an interior track except at its own attaching endpoint.

Each completed track is a proper simple embedded copy of \(\mathbb R\): the
original compact simple arc has two different boundary ends, and the two added
unbounded rays have different directions and are disjoint. Its one-point
compactification is a Jordan curve on the sphere, so it separates the plane.
All intersections between different completed tracks are the original tile
crossings. There are no new crossings or triple points.

It remains essential to check the exterior faces, rather than merely applying
the source lemma. Radial coordinates about \(o\) identify the exterior with a
circular family of unbounded radial intervals. The added rays divide it into
wedges between consecutive endpoints in their cyclic boundary order. Every
boundary unit edge has exactly one track endpoint, at its midpoint. Consequently
the boundary interval between two consecutive endpoints contains exactly one
tiling boundary vertex, including when that vertex is a polygon corner or a
collinear subdivision vertex. This interval contains no other endpoint.

The wedge therefore glues to the truncated star of that single vertex.
Different vertex stars cannot become connected through the exterior: changing
the angular interval requires crossing one of the radial rays. There is no
passage through a point at infinity in the plane. These wedges add neither a
new face nor an identification of different existing faces.

An exterior ray is part of the same track segment as its adjacent interior
piece, up to the nearest tile-center crossing. Its dual edge is the boundary
unit-edge adjacency already identified above, not an additional shortcut or a
new parallel edge. Thus the full plane dual graph is exactly the tiling
one-skeleton as an abstract graph. This addresses both possible failures:
merging boundary cells and adding exterior adjacencies.

## DG1, step 3: identify individual curves with lift thresholds

Here is an explicit bridge for the candidate's nested-sign statement. Along a
color-\(i\) edge write the lower \(i\)-coordinate of its endpoint as \(r-1\).
Opposite color-\(i\) edges of a tile have the same lower \(i\)-coordinate,
because passing between them adds or subtracts \(e_j\), \(j\neq i\).
Following a single color-\(i\) track consequently preserves this integer \(r\).

On either boundary chain from \(0\) to \(\sum m_iv_i\), the \(m_i\)
color-\(i\) edges have lower \(i\)-coordinates \(0,\ldots,m_i-1\).
A track ends on each of the two corresponding color-\(i\) boundary sides.
Thus the \(m_i\) tracks have the distinct threshold labels
\(r=1,\ldots,m_i\), and a track of label \(r\) crosses exactly those
color-\(i\) edges whose lift passes between \(r-1\) and \(r\).

The completed track separates the vertex \(0\) from the opposite marked
vertex. Orient its side sign to be positive at the latter vertex. At \(0\)
the sign is zero. Along any edge path from \(0\), this sign toggles exactly
when \(a_i\) crosses the threshold \(r\). Since the one-skeleton is connected,
the positive sign at any vertex \(x\) is precisely
\[
{\bf1}_{\{a_i(x)\geq r\}}.
\]
This also establishes \(0\leq a_i(x)\leq m_i\); equivalently, these are
strip ranks in the standard lift dictionary. The positive sign sets at
vertices are the initial segments \(\{1,\ldots,a_i(x)\}\).

In particular, the number of color-\(i\) completed tracks separating the cells
of \(x\) and \(y\) is exactly \(|a_i(y)-a_i(x)|\). The statement is stronger
than an unsupported assertion that disjoint proper curves have a common order:
the marked boundary chains and the constant threshold label identify the
specific order and orientation required here.

An additive integer kernel translation of all lifts would not change the
distance formula or coordinate order. It would only change the literal
phrase “sizes \(a_i\)” unless the normalization is specified.

## DG1, step 4: distance and direction

Apply the source lemma to the two completed-arrangement cells. A dual path
crossing a separating proper track must cross it at least once. A path between
two points on the same side cannot cross that track an odd number of times.
Since each track is crossed at most once, the lemma's path crosses exactly
the separating tracks, each once. By the cell/edge correspondence this is an
available tiling edge path of length
\[
\sum_i |a_i(y)-a_i(x)|.
\]

Every tiling edge changes one lift coordinate by one. Hence every tiling edge
path has length at least the displayed sum. The exhibited path attains it,
proving the proposed graph-distance formula.

When all coordinate differences are nonnegative, let \(p_i,q_i\) be the counts
of positive and negative color-\(i\) steps of this path. The endpoint lift
gives \(p_i-q_i=a_i(y)-a_i(x)\), whereas its crossings give
\(p_i+q_i=|a_i(y)-a_i(x)|\). Thus \(q_i=0\) for every \(i\).
The shortest path is positive. This argument also covers equal coordinates:
no edge of that color is used. It does not require a directed version of the
source theorem.

## DG2: exact quantitative reconstruction

Assume the candidate's \(VJ=I_2\), \(Vc=0\), fixed direction \(u\), and
\[
0<\kappa\leq J_i u\leq K,\qquad
x_{k+1}-x_k=\ell u,\qquad
\|a(w_k)-(Jx_k+c)\|_\infty\leq E,\qquad
\kappa\ell\geq2E.
\]
Here \(E\geq0\) and \(\ell\geq0\), as intended for an error and a mesh
length. Set \(e_k=a(w_k)-(Jx_k+c)\). Then
\[
\Delta a_i=\ell J_i u+e_{k+1,i}-e_{k,i},
\qquad
0\leq\kappa\ell-2E\leq\Delta a_i\leq K\ell+2E.
\]
DG1 therefore supplies a positive path between each successive pair.

For a point \(z\) on such a path, extend the lift linearly on each edge and
write it as \(h(z)\). Positivity gives
\[
0\leq h_i(z)-a_i(w_k)\leq\Delta a_i\leq K\ell+2E.
\]
With \(M_V=\sum_i\|v_i\|_2\),
\[
\|w_k-x_k\|_2=\|Ve_k\|_2\leq M_V E
\]
and
\[
\|z-w_k\|_2
 \leq\sum_i\|v_i\|_2|h_i(z)-a_i(w_k)|
 \leq M_V(K\ell+2E).
\]
The triangle inequality gives exactly
\[
\|z-x_k\|_2\leq M_V(K\ell+3E).
\]
This proof holds on edge interiors as well as vertices. Because \(x_k\)
belongs to the target segment, it also bounds the distance to that segment.
The graph length is \(\sum_i\Delta a_i\), and even the Euclidean edge
arclength is the endpoint-dependent sum
\(\sum_i\|v_i\|_2\Delta a_i\). Every nonzero edge advances the physical first
coordinate by one, so a positive concatenation cannot create a nontrivial
self-intersection. This observation does not ensure compatibility with paths
chosen for other sides.

Equality \(\kappa\ell=2E\) is allowed. Some coordinates can have zero
increment and, in a degenerate sampled step, both vertices can agree; the
zero-edge path is valid. If \(E=\ell=0\), all target points and all vertices
agree and the stated zero bound holds. These cases do not require strict
positivity of every increment.

## DG2: sampling, buffers and scale quantifiers

The last paragraph is correct with the following explicit conventional
quantifiers. Fix \(D,J,u,\kappa,K\), an affine-error constant \(C\), and a
bounded tile-to-vertex sampling constant \(C_0\). Suppose
\[
0<\varepsilon\leq1,\qquad E\leq C\varepsilon L+C_0.
\]
Choose a fixed \(A\geq\max\{1,4C/\kappa\}\) and
\[
\ell=A\sqrt\varepsilon L,\qquad
L\geq\frac{4C_0}{\kappa A\sqrt\varepsilon}.
\]
Then \(\kappa\ell\geq2E\). The exact bound above is at most a constant,
depending on these fixed data, times \(\sqrt\varepsilon L\). No limit in
which \(\kappa\) tends to zero is uniform in this argument.

For a tile containing \(x_k\), its diameter is at most
\(\max_{i,j}(\|v_i\|_2+\|v_j\|_2)\). The affine interpolation of its lifted
vertices has each coordinate within one of the corresponding coordinate at
any tile vertex. Thus a continuous uniform lift estimate at \(x_k\) allows
choosing a vertex with only an added constant error. If instead the estimate
is given at vertices and compared to \(Jx_k+c\), the sampling correction is
bounded by this tile diameter times a row norm of \(J\). Both readings yield
the stated \(C_0=O_{D,J}(1)\).

Let \(B=M_V(K\ell+3E)\). To keep the path in a prescribed region, the
closed \(B\)-neighborhood of the entire target segment must be contained in
that region. This condition includes all sample points and covers every
subpath; an endpoint-only buffer does not suffice for an arbitrary nonconvex
region. At the scales just stated, any larger fixed multiple of
\(\sqrt\varepsilon L\) gives the required buffer. Without it, DG2 remains a
valid construction in the full tiling and says nothing about confinement to a
chosen subregion.

The candidate explicitly recognizes this buffer requirement and the fixed
positive margin. Its compact notation \(O_{D,J}\) should nevertheless be
read as conditional on the other data just fixed, or be expanded to display
their dependence. A vanishing \(\varepsilon\) requires a simultaneous
large-scale threshold at least of order \(1/\sqrt\varepsilon\) when the
sampling constant is nonzero. Taking \(\varepsilon=0\) in the square-root
choice without treating sampling separately is not an asserted consequence.

## Degenerate cases and proposed stronger conclusions challenged

- Missing colors cause no DG1 problem if at least two active colors remain:
  their lifts are constant and their separating-curve counts are zero.
  With only one active direction, \(Z\) is a segment rather than a tiled
  disk; the radial proof has no interior point. A direct one-dimensional
  path statement would be trivial, but is outside this proof's disk domain.
- The source allows noncrossing pairs. There is no need to manufacture
  crossings at infinity, quotient parallel curves, or infer a projective
  arrangement having the same finite dual.
- Boundary subdivision vertices, polygon corners, and arbitrary interior
  valences are all covered by the sector and wedge proof. The exterior is
  the plane, with no permitted passage through its compactifying point.
- DG1 is about the whole tiling graph. Its shortest path need not be a path
  in an arbitrary selected patch. DG2 needs its buffer to obtain a patch
  confinement statement.
- Equal endpoint coordinates and repeated sample vertices are harmless.
  A family of separately localized side paths still need not have matching
  corner lifts, ordered intersections, compatible phases or a common simple
  closed boundary.
- The displayed margin excludes a zero or negative \(J_i u\). No bound
  uniform near that excluded stratum, closed-annulus extension, preservation
  of inserted interiors, EAI, profile recovery, or unrestricted exponent is
  established by these arguments.
