### Retained supports and the entire residual set

For fixed odd m=2t+1, the finite grid has fourfold integer points in the central diamond and triple integer points in the rest of the axis square. The counts remain

`t4=(m^2+1)/2`, `t3=(m^2-1)/2`.

The retained 3-by-3 translates have fixed rational phases. Their closures lie in the diamond; only cells in a band of bounded width can meet the diamond and fail retention. Thus their number is `K_m=2t^2/9-O(t)=m^2/18-O(m)`. Every omitted integer fourfold point is in such a boundary band, so there are O(m) residual points. This uses the lattice's bounded point density as well as the band's O(m) area; it is not inferred from the small finite run.

The original grid vertices lie at integer or half-integer coordinates, while the tiling edges have the specified fractional phases. All vertices therefore have positive clearance from the edges, with a phase-dependent lower bound independent of m. Likewise, the finitely many line-port/corner patterns of one tile have positive clearance. One can shrink and round retained rectangles in sufficiently thin collars without losing a grid crossing, changing the paired boundary word, or introducing a tangent/corner port. The choices can be convex rounded rectangles, hence each line still meets a support in at most one proper arc. Neighboring closed supports become disjoint. These are construction choices made once, not new counted parameters.

The counted triple disks are then fixed about all t3 triple points, outside the diamond. They can be chosen disjoint from the large supports, from one another, from every other vertex and from all nonincident lines. Their local paired word is the complete three-strand word, so the two simple resolutions are the appropriate two choices.
