### Sign cells and the interface are global, not merely sidewise

Let each wire be a continuous x-monotone graph f_i, with one transverse
crossing per pair and no triple crossing. For a prescribed above-set T,

i in T and j outside T. Each inequality changes truth once, hence is a
half-line. Their intersection is an interval. At any feasible x the
vertical fiber is the interval between max of the outside wires and
min of the inside wires. Continuous boundaries join those fibers into
one connected cell. Empty/all T have one infinite fiber endpoint and
give the north/south cases. There cannot be two cells with one sign set.

Restricting all other strict inequalities to wire i also gives an
interval of its parameter. Therefore a cell has at most one edge of
each wire label. At the crossing-free vertical interface the cells have
exactly the prefixes of its top-to-bottom order as above-sets. A cell
having a point on one side and a nonprefix above-set cannot cross the
interface; connectedness would give an interface point. These facts
justify localization of WHOLE cells and count edge labels without
accidentally adding two pieces of one edge twice.

If same-color wires do not cross on the right, a right cell intersects
each color in a prefix of its fixed order. There is at most one possible
label to add and one to remove per color. A WHOLLY right cell therefore
has both directed degrees at most the number of colors. This is not a
claim about an arbitrary interface cell: its left boundary may add edges.
