## 1. Canonical projected tracks and internal data

Take an original reduced word T on n labels. Immediately before any
of its swaps, its current list of colors is unchanged if all earlier
equal-color swaps have been omitted: such an omission swaps identical
colors. Every retained swap therefore still acts at its original
position index. The retained word is P(T), a reduced word for the
block-reversal permutation, by the accepted DC construction.

Name its tracks (i,r), with r the initial rank inside color i.
Equal-color tracks never cross in P(T), so r remains their current
same-color rank. Each (i,r) crosses EACH track of another color once
and no track of its own color. This last assertion concerns projected
rank identities, not original label identities after smoothing:
the retained block-permutation word is reduced and its inversion
pairs are exactly the differently colored projected label pairs.

The directed list of retained vertices on (i,r), each named by its
other projected track, has length a_i and is canonical in the
commutation class Q=P(T). Its gap index j, 0<=j<=a_i, means that
exactly j retained vertices precede the gap. Endpoint gaps count;
metric crossing times or a chosen global sweep are not code data.

Restrict the ORIGINAL diagram to the m_i original labels in block i,
by deleting all other original labels. An internal crossing was
adjacent before deletion and is still adjacent after deletion.
Every internal pair crosses exactly once and the final order is
reversed. This gives a reduced full-reversal word A_i.

A_i is well defined on full commutation classes. Exchanging two
disjoint original swaps either has no effect on this restriction,
leaves just one event, or exchanges two surviving disjoint adjacent
pairs in the restricted list. The last two restricted generators
commute. There is no arbitrary labeling or extra linear extension.

In a representative of A_i, smooth every crossing. At an event s_r
put a + port on rank r and a - port on rank r+1. The full directed
symbol list on every rank is invariant: a commutation exchanges
s_r,s_s with |r-s|>1, which have disjoint rank pairs. It changes no
per-rank port order. In particular

    x_r(A_i)=number of + ports on rank r,
    sum_(r=1)^(m_i-1) x_r(A_i)=binom(m_i,2).            (1)

Use the ID (r,l) for the lth + port on rank r. Its partner is the
lth - port on rank r+1. This forced pairing follows either from the
actual word or from DC: the two ends of each contact share one
time in a representative, so the repeated-contact orders agree.
Thus A_i supplies the entire ordered contact-ID list on each rank,
including the relative order of + contacts to rank r+1 and -
contacts to rank r-1. This is more data than the counts x_r alone.

Deleting other colors does not change a current same-color rank.
Consequently the contact-ID lists of A_i are exactly the lists of
erased contacts along projected tracks (i,r), after forgetting the
retained other-color vertices. These are the accepted V internal
lists, with their event IDs made explicit. We do NOT replace them
by independently chosen binary words or by an arbitrary total order
of all contacts.

## 2. Proof of OP1: adjacency forces equal crossed sets

Fix an erased equal-color event in a representative T. Suppose its
two current same-color ranks are r,r+1 in color i. They were adjacent
in the FULL instantaneous original position list. Omit all prior
equal-color events. The resulting projected current list has the
same colors in the same positions as that original list. The two
projected tracks occupying these positions are therefore exactly
(i,r),(i,r+1), and are adjacent in this projected current list.

Let t be ANY projected track of a different color. Initially it was
on the same side of both (i,r) and (i,r+1): the initial color-i block
was consecutive. At the chosen instant it is again on the same
side of both, since no track can lie between adjacent positions.

A pair of projected tracks crosses at most once. Hence its current
relative order differs from its initial relative order if and only
if that pair has already crossed. Applying this to t with each
of (i,r),(i,r+1), the initial-side and current-side equalities give

    t crossed (i,r) before this contact
      iff t crossed (i,r+1) before this contact.       (2)

This holds for every other-color projected t, proving equality of
the crossed-track SETS. Their cardinalities are the two gap indices,
so those indices agree.

The representative instant is only a proof device. The conclusion
is about canonical gap indices and prefix sets of Q, which do not
depend on a chosen sweep. It therefore applies to the invariant
port decoration of every original commutation class.

The consecutive-block hypothesis matters. If an external track
initially lies BETWEEN two tracks being compared, equality of
current sides need not imply equality of crossed indicators. We do
not apply OP1 to arbitrary nonconsecutive color classes, repeated
crossings, arbitrary local recolorings or arbitrary disk boundaries.

## 3. Common-prefix refinement and complete reconstruction

For fixed Q, let L_(i,r) be the canonical ordered list of other-color
projected tracks crossed by (i,r). Let P_(i,r)(j) be the SET of the
first j entries, 0<=j<=a_i. Define, for 1<=r<m_i,

    D_(i,r)(Q)={j: P_(i,r)(j)=P_(i,r+1)(j)},
    b_(i,r)(Q)=|D_(i,r)(Q)|.                           (3)

The two sets always include j=0 and j=a_i; these are the SAME index
when a_i=0. Thus b>=1, and b>=2 if a_i>0. It is at most a_i+1.
Only necessary conditions are encoded. A common prefix index is
not asserted to admit a contact, let alone an arbitrary interior.

For a vector of nonnegative integer parameters c_1,...,c_(m-1), set

    V_m(c_1,...,c_(m-1))
       =sum_(A in C(m)) product_(r=1)^(m-1) F_(c_r)(x_r(A)). (4)

Use V_1(empty)=1. Its constant-parameter specialization is V_m(a)
from OP2. For a fixed actual Q and fixed A_1,...,A_D, record ONLY
the + ports. On (i,r), allocate its x_r(A_i) ordered + ports among
the b_(i,r) allowed gaps in (3). The allocation is an ordered weak
composition, with exactly

    binom(b_(i,r)+x_r(A_i)-1,x_r(A_i))                 (5)

possibilities. This includes repeated contacts in one gap and
x_r=0. Fill the ordered + contact-ID list successively according
to these gap counts. No permutation of contacts is recorded.

For each such + contact ID, put its - partner on (i,r+1) in the
SAME gap INDEX, by OP1. This locates EVERY port. For every rank
the full internal contact-ID list supplied by A_i determines the
relative order of ALL its ports, including opposite signs sharing
a gap. In an actual record, its assigned gap indices are
nondecreasing along that list; if an abstract record violates this
or any other consistency condition, discard it. There is no choice
left in the order inside a gap.

We have recovered the complete DC decorated projected class:
its canonical Q, each gap's ordered ports and their neighbor signs.
The kth-to-kth matching restores contact pairs, and cross-routing
at erased contacts restores original labeled strands. Retained
crossings keep their projected through-routing. Following every
initial original label now recovers its full ordered crossing list.

For completeness, these original-strand orders determine the class.
Form the event poset generated by them. Every original representative
is a linear extension. Linear extensions are connected by exchanges
of adjacent incomparable events. Such events share no original
strand; when consecutive in a valid adjacent-swap word, their
position intervals are disjoint, since otherwise they share a
current label. The exchange is therefore an allowed commutation
and leaves a valid word. This recovers the original class with no
hidden global order, matching factorial or planar metric choice.

Q and every A_i are invariant. The + gap counts are a subset of the
already invariant DC full decoration. The resulting map is thus
well defined and injective on original COMMUTATION CLASSES. It is
not just an injection on selected representative words.

Summing the possible records gives the stronger finite bound

    C(n)<=sum_(Q in B(m_1,...,m_D))
                product_i V_(m_i)(b_(i,1)(Q)-1,...,
                                  b_(i,m_i-1)(Q)-1). (6)

Here Q ranges over classes, not reduced words. Since b_(i,r)-1<=a_i
and F_c(x) is nondecreasing in c, (6) implies OP2a. Its lower bound
is the actual accepted DC section. The construction does not assert
that independently chosen Q,A_i or weak compositions are realizable;
all such invalid records only enlarge the upper bound.

Use the initial projected labels A_1,A_2,B,C at positions1,2,3,4.

    s_2 s_3 s_2 s_1 s_2

takes 1234 to4312, the required block reversal with A internally

their prefix sets agree only at j=0,2, and b=2 instead of a+1=3.

The internal A class is the single s_1, so it has one + contact.
Only gap0 or gap2 can be its location. Both occur: prefix the

Thus this Q has exactly two preimages, while the uniform V_2(2)
bound is three. This is a hand reconstruction for one Q, not
an enumeration, asymptotic evidence or proof of sufficiency of

## 4. Closed finite bounds and all boundary conventions

For integers u>=v+2 and a>=0,

    F_a(u-1)F_a(v+1)/[F_a(u)F_a(v)]
       =[u/(a+u)] [(a+v+1)/(v+1)]>=1,                 (7)

because the cross-multiplied difference is a(u-v-1)>=0.
This includes a=0, where every F_0(x)=1.

There are m-1 coordinates x_r in (1), summing to m(m-1)/2.
Balancing two coordinates differing by at least2 preserves their
sum, does not decrease their product of F values by (7), and
strictly decreases the sum of their squares. At termination all
coordinates differ by at most1 and have mean m/2.

If m=2t, all coordinates are t. If m=2t+1, exactly t coordinates
are t and t are t+1. These are upper-bound tuples; their
realizability as a class is unnecessary. This proves OP2b.

For OP2c, fix Q and all A_i, and ignore the individual + port totals
temporarily. The S + ports have nonnegative counts in the E_+
named canonical gaps of all tracks r<m_i. Such arrays have
binom(E_++S-1,S) possibilities. The actual arrays have the required
x_r sums; allowing all arrays only increases the bound. Once an
array is actual, A_i determines the contact order and OP1 supplies
the missing - ends exactly as above. Multiplying by the number
product C(m_i) of internal records proves OP2c. If S>0, at least
one m_i>=2, so E_+>=1 and its binomial is defined. If S=0 there
are no internal contacts and exactly one empty placement record;
the separately defined H=1 avoids a fictitious binom(-1,0).

All other endpoints are explicit:

* D=1: a=0, B(n)=1 and V_n(0)=C(n), so OP2a is equality.
  The weaker auxiliary bounds OP2b/c need not be equality.
* All m_i=1: every internal product is empty, S=E_+=0,
  V_1=1 and B(1,...,1)=C(n), again equality.
* m=2: x_1=1, so V_2(a)=a+1 exactly.
* Auxiliary n=1 has D=1,m_1=1 and the same empty conventions.
  Empty blocks, D=0 and n=0 are excluded.

For comparison with the old V weight, put x_0=x_m=0. Its all-port
degree on rank r is k_r=x_(r-1)+x_r. For r<m, k_r>=x_r and
F_a is nondecreasing, while the last rank contributes an extra

    V_m(a)<=W_m(a).                                   (8)

For m=2,a>0 this is strictly a+1<(a+1)^2. OP2 is therefore a

## 5. Normalize the finite code: proof of OP3

Fix D>=2, and choose balanced m_i in{floor(n/D),ceil(n/D)} summing
to n. All m_i are nonempty for large n. Accepted monotonicity and
all-integer G2 give

    n^-2 log2 B(m_1,...,m_D)->ell_D.                  (9)

The separately accepted L limit, applied at m_i->infinity, gives

    n^-2 sum_i log2 C(m_i)
      =sum_i (m_i/n)^2 [log2 C(m_i)/m_i^2]->L/D.      (10)

At this fixed D, m_i/n->1/D. Hence

    S/n^2 ->1/(2D),
    E_+/n^2 ->1-1/D,
    (E_++S-1)/n^2 ->1-1/(2D).                       (11)

For example, the exact finite identity

    E_+=n^2-sum_i m_i^2+(2-D)n-D

verifies that its lower-order terms are O_D(n). This identity also
holds at the finite empty-placement endpoints above.

For integers M>=1,0<=s<=M, the binomial theorem at t=s/M yields

    log2 binom(M,s)<=M h_2(s/M),                     (12)

with the endpoint cases immediate. Apply it to
M=E_++S-1,s=S, which are in range for large balanced n.
By (11) and continuity of h_2,

    limsup_n n^-2 log2 H
       <=(1-1/(2D)) h_2(1/(2D-1))=f_D.              (13)

Taking limsups in OP2c and using (9),(10),(13) gives

    L<=ell_D+L/D+f_D.

The lower bound ell_D<=L is already supplied by DC/G.
Rearranging for D>1 proves OP3b and substituting L<U proves
OP3a. The sharper common-prefix expression (6) or OP2b can
only improve a finite upper estimate; no asymptotic distribution
of those profiles was assumed.

To compare the explicit direction errors, put p=1/D. Direct
entropy algebra gives

    (1-p/2)h_2(p/(2-p))
       =h_2(p)-h_2(p/2)+p.                           (14)

Strict concavity of h_2 on(0,1) and its chord between0 and1/2
give h_2(p/2)>p for0<p<1. Thus f_D<h_2(1/D) for D>=2.

Finally -(1-p)log2(1-p)=O(p) as p->0, whence
h_2(p)=p log2(1/p)+O(p). Apply this also at p/2 in (14):

    f_D=(log2 D)/(2D)+O(1/D).

This proves OP3c, so even after adding L/D or U/D the leading
logarithmic direction error is halved. There is no exchange of
D and n limits and no assertion for D=D(n) with a uniform side
threshold.

## 6. Exact obstruction to an old degree-only improvement

This auxiliary argument concerns the OLD placement product for
one internal class; it is not a lower bound for a typical class
or for all the weighted mass.

Let m=2t>=2. Put

    O=s_1 s_3 ... s_(m-1),
    E=s_2 s_4 ... s_(m-2).

The word (OE)^t has every generator s_r exactly t times. Track the
POSITION of an original label through one OE layer. Its position
map c sends odd j<m-1 to j+2, m-1 to m, even j>2 to j-2, and2 to1.
Thus c is the cycle

    (1,3,5,...,m-1,m,m-2,...,2).

In that cycle, entries t places apart add to m+1, so c^t sends
every j to m+1-j. The word therefore gives the longest permutation.
Its length is t[(t)+(t-1)]=binom(m,2), its inversion length, so
it is reduced. It defines an ACTUAL commutation class A_* with
x_r=t for all r.

After full smoothing its old per-rank degrees are

    k_1=k_m=t, and k_r=m for2<=r<=m-1.

Consequently its old placement product is

    R=F_a(t)^2 F_a(m)^(m-2),

whereas the old mean-degree Jensen bound is

    J_old=F_a(m-1)^m.

The old balancing inequality gives R<=J_old. Since F_a is
nondecreasing and F_a(t)>=1,

    J_old/R <=[F_a(m)/F_a(t)]^2
              <=F_a(m)^2<=2^(2(a+m)).                (15)

The final inequality is binom(a+m,m)<=2^(a+m).
Thus

    0<=log2 J_old-log2 R<=2(a+m).

For a=O(m), the gap is O(m), not a positive constant times m^2.
It is therefore impossible to improve that OLD PER-CLASS bound
uniformly over actual internal classes by a fixed 2^(-delta m^2)
factor at these parameters. At the present fixed-D balanced
parameters a~(D-1)m, this obstruction applies for each fixed D.

It does not prove W_m(a) has the old exponent: the single class
may carry little of the weighted sum, while C(m) itself has
quadratic entropy. Distributional estimates might still improve
W_m. The one-sided OP1 code succeeds by removing redundant ends,
rather than by claiming that old degree tuples cannot nearly
balance.
