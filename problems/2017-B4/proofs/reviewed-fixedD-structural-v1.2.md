## 1. Exact integer-parallelogram reformulation

Fix D>=2 and set `v_i=(1,i-1)`, i=1,...,D. Put

    Z_p = sum_i [0,p v_i],
    V = [v_1 ... v_D].

Use unit parallelograms generated by v_i,v_j, i<j, with vertices in Z^2.
Their areas are j-i and their horizontal edge displacements are 1.
The candidate dictionary is a bijection between their edge-to-edge
tilings of Z_p and the D-color block-reversal commutation classes counted
by B_D(p). Thus the deformation from regular rhombi to these integer
parallelograms changes geometry but preserves the count.

One direction is especially explicit. Start with the lower boundary path
`1^p 2^p ... D^p`. Each adjacent inversion-creating swap `i j -> j i`, i<j,
replaces the lower two-edge boundary of one positive-area parallelogram
by its upper two-edge boundary. All edges have positive x displacement.
The path rises pointwise, and the added shell is disjoint from earlier
shell interiors. A reduced block reversal therefore tiles Z_p. Disjoint
swaps change only shelling order and give the same tiling.

For the converse, follow the usual de Bruijn curves through opposite
edges of color i. Along such a curve orient the traversal so
`ell_i(x)=det(v_i,x)` increases. Traversing an ij tile changes ell_i by
`|det(v_i,v_j)|>0`, so no curve is closed. It connects the two opposite
color-i sides of Z_p. At a crossing between colors i<j, the oriented
tangents can be chosen as `+v_j` and `-v_i`; their determinant is positive.
Consequently every intersection of that pair of oriented curves has the
same local intersection sign. Proper arcs in a disk have algebraic
intersection 0 or +/-1, determined by endpoint alternation. Different
colors have alternating endpoints on Z_p, so each such pair crosses
exactly once; same-color curves do not cross. Boundary ranks are fixed.
This gives precisely the full D-bundle marked arrangement. Its crossing
orders determine its commutation class, and the geometric tiling recovers
the same curves. This is also the familiar zonogon/wiring dictionary in
the project's attributed sources, but the above explains the integer
deformation's hypotheses.

No interior integer vertex can lie strictly inside a tile edge, since
that edge has horizontal displacement 1. The integer-origin local model
therefore has no hidden subdivision of a primitive edge. We still require
nonoverlap and face-to-face incidence; arbitrary translated packings with
T-junctions are not silently added to the count.

## 2. Finite local alphabet and vector lift

For fixed D, the prototiles have uniformly bounded diameter. In every
unit lattice cell record all incident prototiles by their type and
integer origin relative to that cell. Only finitely many possibilities
exist. Agreement of these records over a bounded neighborhood, together
with a local exact-cover check inside the cell, specifies the tiling.
Conversely the tiling gives one such record at every cell. This is a
finite-alphabet, finite-range lattice model; the exact polygonal boundary
is imposed by the outside-empty records, including cut cells. It is not
an argument that the fixed boundary has negligible entropy cost.

Lift an edge v_i to e_i in R^D, starting at 0 at the initial corner.
Every tile boundary has zero lifted sum, and the tiled disk is simply
connected, so the lift a is well-defined and continuous after affine
interpolation on each tile. It satisfies `V a(x)=x`. All vertex lift
coordinates lie between 0 and p, as is also visible from the p parallel
de Bruijn curves per color.

On a tile of type ij, writing k_i=i-1, k_j=j-1, its gradient J has rows

    J_i=(k_j,-1)/(k_j-k_i),
    J_j=(-k_i,1)/(k_j-k_i),
    J_r=0 for r not in {i,j}.

In particular all its ordered 2x2 row minors are nonnegative; the ij
minor is `1/(j-i)`. Every coordinate gradient is bounded by a constant
depending only on D. The normalized lifts

    h_T(x)=a_T(p x)/p,   x in Z_1,

are uniformly Lipschitz and have the same boundary lift h_b. Their union
has compact closure X_D in the uniform norm by Arzela--Ascoli.

The minor constraint passes to uniform limits with these derivative
bounds. For Lipschitz f,g,

    det(grad f,grad g)
      = partial_1(f partial_2 g)-partial_2(f partial_1 g)

in distributions. Uniform convergence of f and weak-* convergence of
the bounded derivatives of g pass the right side to the limit. Since
each approximating minor is nonnegative, every limit minor is a
nonnegative distribution, hence nonnegative almost everywhere. Thus

    grad h(x) in K_D almost everywhere,
    K_D={J: VJ=I_2, det(J_i,J_j)>=0 for i<j}

is a necessary condition for h in X_D. Sufficiency for arbitrary
non-affine h is NOT asserted.

The slope set K_D itself is compact. Write Delta_ij=det(J_i,J_j), with
the antisymmetric convention for i>j. Cauchy--Binet gives
`1=sum_(i<j)(j-i)Delta_ij`, so all nonnegative ordered minors are
bounded. The identity VJ=I also gives
`J_i=(sum_k (k-1)Delta_ik, -sum_k Delta_ik)`, by taking determinants
against the two rows of VJ. Thus all entries of J are bounded, and K_D
is closed.

For completeness, a full Z_p tiling has exactly p^2 tiles of every pair
type ij. Integration of the ij minor over Z_p therefore gives p^2, and
over normalized Z_1 gives 1. These integral identities also follow from
the common boundary by the same divergence formula.

## 3. Exact D=4 obstruction to convex mixing of fixed-frame profiles

Let D=4 in the integer model. Begin with lower path 1234 and let a
generator s_k interchange positions k,k+1. Two reduced reversal words are

    T_A: 3,2,1,2,3,2,
    T_B: 1,2,3,2,1,2.

Their path states are, respectively,

    1234 -> 1243 -> 1423 -> 4123 -> 4213 -> 4231 -> 4321,
    1234 -> 2134 -> 2314 -> 2341 -> 2431 -> 4231 -> 4321.

In T_A the 12 swap has prefix 4, so its tile12 has origin v_4=(1,3).
In T_B the 34 swap has prefix 2, so its tile34 has origin v_2=(1,1).
Both tile interiors contain `z=(2,7/2)`, the center of each tile. The
two full tilings have the SAME prescribed zonogon boundary lift.

On an open neighborhood of z, their lift gradients are J12 and J34.
The average gradient has rows

    (1/2,-1/2), (0,1/2), (3/2,-1/2), (-1,1/2).

Its minor for rows 1 and 4 is `-1/4`. Therefore the average of these
two admissible fixed-frame height profiles is not in X_4: it cannot
even be a uniform limit of finer tilings with the uniform derivative
bound. Dilation and subdivision of each tile show that each individual
profile IS such a limit along integer dilations.

The phenomenon is not caused by unequal tile lengths. For the regular
unit octagon choose

    v1=(1,0), v2=(c,c), v3=(0,1), v4=(-c,c), c=sqrt(2)/2.

Use the same two words and the same designated translated tiles. Both
contain `z=(1/2,5/4)` in their interiors. For tile12+v4 the coefficients
of v1,v2 are

    sqrt(2)-3/4,   5sqrt(2)/4-1;

for tile34+v2 the coefficients of v3,v4 are

    7/4-sqrt(2),   1-c.

All four coefficients lie strictly between 0 and 1. More generally,
for any ordered vectors with these two tile interiors overlapping,

    det(((J12+J34)/2)_1, ((J12+J34)/2)_4)
      = -det(v2,v3)/(4 det(v1,v2) det(v3,v4)) < 0.

This is an obstruction to assuming convexity of the admissible profile
set in codimension two. It does NOT refute (H), a nonconvex variational
principle, or a suitable interpolation theorem within an admissible
stratum. In particular, it does not replace the separate source audit
with a blanket dismissal of variational methods.

## 4. A positive lemma: bounded weak-order envelopes at fixed D

Consider words with m_i occurrences of color i. Label occurrences of a
color by increasing rank, so a word is a shuffle preserving each color's
internal rank order. Initial label order is by color and then rank.
Let Inv(w) be its inverted pairs of initially ordered labels. Only pairs
of distinct colors can be inverted. Order these shuffles by inclusion of
inversion sets, i.e. the weak-order interval from the sorted color word
to its block reversal.

**Envelope lemma.** Let A be any finite family of shuffles
containing a reference word w0. Suppose every label has its position in
each w in A within r of its position in w0. Then the join and meet of A
exist in this shuffle interval and displace every label from w0 by at
most `2(D-1)r`.

Here is a direct proof, including the needed lattice fact. Let U be the
union of the inversion sets and let S be its transitive closure on
increasing initial labels. An inversion set is closed and coclosed: for
a<b<c, `(a,b),(b,c)` imply `(a,c)`, and `(a,c)` implies at least one of
`(a,b),(b,c)`. The set S is closed by definition. To check coclosure,
take a path `a=x0<x1<...<xk=c` of U edges and an intermediate label b.
If b is a path vertex, closure gives the assertion. Otherwise b lies
between x_j and x_(j+1). This edge belongs to one original inversion
set; its coclosure gives `(x_j,b)` or `(b,x_(j+1))`. The corresponding
prefix or suffix of the path proves `(a,b)` or `(b,c)` in S.

Any closed and coclosed set of pairs is an inversion set: orient every
pair by whether it is inverted. The only possible directed 3-cycles
would violate closure or coclosure, so this tournament is a total order.
Moreover every edge of U strictly increases the initial color. The same
is true along paths, so S contains no same-color inversion, and a path
has at most D-1 edges. Thus S is the join in the shuffle interval.

If an edge `(a,b)` in U is not inverted in w0, then

    0 < pos_w0(b)-pos_w0(a) <= 2r.

An edge already inverted in w0 decreases the reference position along
the path. Consequently any new inversion of S has its two reference
positions at distance at most `R=2(D-1)r`. Since w0 is in A, every old
reference inversion is retained. Thus comparisons between labels whose
reference positions differ by more than R are unchanged. A label can
move at most R positions. This proves the join bound.

For the meet, reverse the word and reverse the rank names inside every
color. This is an order-reversing involution of the shuffle interval:
it complements cross-color inversions up to that rank renaming and
preserves all position-displacement bounds. Apply the join result.

The bound is uniform in the number of occurrences of each color. The
restriction to fixed D is decisive: an inversion chain cannot propagate
through arbitrarily many distinct initial colors.

## 5. What the envelope lemma provides geometrically

In the integer model, the x coordinate of a path vertex equals its word
position. If the label displacement between two words is at most R,
each prefix color count differs by at most R, and their vector lifts
and physical y coordinates differ by O_D(R). Thus the envelopes remain
in a band of width O_D(r) around their reference path.

If P<=Q in this weak order, a chain of inversion-creating adjacent swaps
from P to Q fills the region between their monotone lattice paths by
positive parallelograms. The paths rise pointwise and shell disjoint
tile interiors. Consequently any fixed patch between two comparable
paths can be padded to a lower meet and an upper join. The padding has
area O_D(r L) for paths of length O(L). For a fixed inner boundary this
is an injective padding operation, because the inner patch is recovered
by restriction. If the inner boundary varies, its word costs only
O_D(L) bits to record; this is subquadratic.

The conversion from prefix counts needs an additional fixed gap bound.
Let a reference word w0 of length L have the same content as every
family word. For each active color i, let t_i(k) be the reference
position of its kth occurrence, and add sentinels t_i(0)=0 and
t_i(m_i+1)=L+1. Assume every consecutive gap, including these two
end gaps, is at most a fixed integer G. A fixed periodic all-active-color
reference supplies such a G after restriction to a sufficiently long
word. G belongs to that reference, not just to D.

If every prefix color count differs from w0 by at most an integer r>=0,
the position t of label(i,k) in any family word satisfies

    t >= t_i(k-r) when k-r>=1,
    t <= t_i(k+r) when k+r<=m_i.

These follow by applying the prefix-count bound at t and t-1,
respectively. If a rank passes an endpoint, use 1<=t<=L and the
sentinel gaps. In every case |t-t_i(k)|<=G*r; r=0 gives identical words.
Consequently Section4 applies with ranked displacement G*r, giving
join/meet displacement at most 2(D-1)G*r, a physical band O_D(G*r),
and padding area O_D(G*r*L).

For a real prefix bound epsilon L use r=ceil(epsilon L), obtaining
band O_(D,G)(epsilon L+1) and area O_(D,G)(epsilon L^2+L). Constants
are not uniform over periods with unbounded G or over a disappearing
color density. Prefix-height closeness without this gap assumption,
or without direct ranked-label displacement control, gives no such
padding theorem; FD2-01 supplies an exact counterexample in the repaired
profile note. No claim below removes those qualifications.

The lemma does NOT yet match all four sides of a closed annulus or
control its corner lift offsets. Nor does it prove that arbitrary
near-flat patches have the prescribed periodic boundary. Those are
separate extension obligations recorded in the reduction note.

## 6. Rational admissible slopes have periodic flat backgrounds

The following independently reviewed construction is useful for stating
the local extension target; it is not a proof of (H).

For a rational J in K_D, choose generic phases theta_i and draw the
straight multigrid `J_i x=k+theta_i`. Omit zero rows; parallel row
families are phased to avoid coincidences. Choose phases avoiding triple
intersections. Because J has rank two, the grid cells are bounded.
Its dual complex has a vertex with integer height k for each grid cell
and one ij parallelogram at each crossing. Map dual vertices to V k.
All its tiles have positive orientation: the nonzero grid pair minors
have the same signs as det(v_i,v_j). The map differs from the identity
by a bounded amount, since `VJ=I`. It is proper of degree one. Every
regular face preimage has positive local degree; therefore multiplicity
is exactly one. This yields a plane tiling with lift `Jx+O_J(1)`.

A full-rank integer period lattice P with JP in Z^D preserves the grid.
The dual physical translation is `VJP=P`, so the tiling is periodic.
This identifies a genuine canonical flat boundary family at rational
admissible slopes, including lower-dimensional strata, instead of
reusing one unrelated periodic-grid entropy.

Rational admissible slopes are dense in K_D. To see why the boundary
strata cause no rationality obstruction, let

    (M_eta)_(i,j)=eta^((i-j)^2),   0<eta<1.

This matrix tends to I and all its ordered 2x2 minors are positive:
the off-diagonal exponent difference is `2(j-i)(l-k)>0` for i<j,k<l.
By Cauchy--Binet all 2x2 minors of M_eta J are strictly positive,
because at least one minor of J is positive. For eta sufficiently
small, A=VM_eta J is close to I and det A>0. Hence

    J_eta = M_eta J A^(-1)

has VJ_eta=I and strictly positive ordered minors. Approximate it by
rational matrices in the rational affine space VJ=I, retaining strict
positivity. This gives rational elements arbitrarily close to J.
This local density result does not establish a global fixed-boundary
regularization or an admissible convex mixture of profiles.
