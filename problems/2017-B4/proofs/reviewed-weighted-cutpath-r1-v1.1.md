## 2. An abstract bound from substochastic exit weights

Let L,M,V>1 with L=MV, and suppose

    2+d/V <= L M^d for EVERY integer d>=0.             (2.1)

In the actual forward directed graph assign these weights:

* a unique exit has weight1;
* from a nonunique cell with d middle exits, each of its two extreme
  exits has weight1/(L M^d);
* each of its d middle exits has weight1/(L M^d V).

By(2.1) the total outgoing weight at that nonunique cell is
`(2+d/V)/(L M^d)<=1`; at a unique cell the sum is1. These weights
are defined on the ORIGINAL graph, without a pruned encoding tree or
choices depending on the path's eventual statistics. A terminal sink
requires no outgoing assignment.

For each complete path p let W(p) be the product of its edge weights.
Finite weighted flow, starting with mass1 at north, proves

    sum_p W(p)<=1.                                  (2.2)

For completeness, unfold the finite acyclic graph into the finite tree
of all directed prefixes. Give each prefix its product weight. The sum
of weights of the children of a prefix is at most that prefix's weight.
Induction down to the terminal leaves proves(2.2). Merging prefixes in
the original graph does not identify paths or destroy this bound.
One can also allow mass to be lost: there is no assumption that each
outgoing sum is exactly1. A complete path has exactly n-u nonunique
tail cells, pays the d tolls whose sum is m, and takes k middle exits.
Consequently

    W(p)=L^(-(n-u)) M^(-m) V^(-k).                    (2.3)

Repeat the SAME assignment in the rotated graph. Reversing the path
is a bijection on the complete cutpath set, so the resulting weight
W'(p) is indexed by precisely the same paths and satisfies

    sum_p W'(p)<=1,
    W'(p)=L^(-(n-u')) M^(-m') V^(-k').                (2.4)

No independence between W and W' is assumed. Cauchy--Schwarz gives

    sum_p sqrt(W(p)W'(p))
       <=sqrt((sum_p W(p))(sum_p W'(p)))<=1.           (2.5)

Put U=u+u' and T=k+k'. Since T<=U and V>1, equations(2.3)--(2.4)
and L=MV give, path by path,

    sqrt(W W')
      =L^(-n+U/2) M^(-(m+m')/2) V^(-T/2)
      >=L^(-n) M^(-(m+m'-U)/2)
      >=L^(-n) M^(-3n/4)=K^(-n),                    (2.6)

using M>1 and the SECOND inequality in(1.1) in the last comparison.
The directions of both inequalities are essential. They remain valid
if m+m'-U is negative. Multiplying this per-path lower bound by the
number of paths and using(2.5) proves WC1.

This direct proof sums over all paths at once. It does not multiply
upper estimates in an invalid direction and does not require grouping
six statistics or paying a polynomial number of classes. It uses
integer local degrees in(2.1), not an unproved integer realization
of an arbitrary entropy-relaxation point.
