## 2. Row identities and a convex scalar cone (GR1)

At a differentiability point write J_i=(a_i,b_i), and extend Delta
antisymmetrically: Delta_ki=-Delta_ik and Delta_ii=0. Differentiating
Vh=x gives

    sum_k J_k=(1,0),       sum_k (k-1)J_k=(0,1).

Taking determinants against J_i gives the exact identities

    a_i=sum_k (k-1)Delta_ik,
    b_i=-sum_k Delta_ik.

Cauchy--Binet, including the actual physical determinants j-i, gives

    1=sum_(i<j) (j-i)Delta_ij.

Thus sum_(i<j) Delta_ij<=1. In particular

    ||J_i||_2
      =||sum_k Delta_ik (k-1,-1)||_2
      <=D sum_k |Delta_ik| <=D.

On the convex polygon, a continuous Lipschitz scalar function whose
gradient has norm <=D almost everywhere is D-Lipschitz everywhere.
For completeness one may mollify it on compact interior segments,
integrate the derivative bound there, and then approach the two endpoints
from the interior. This is a scalar convex gradient bound.

For the determinant-one coordinates x=u v_i+s w, w=(0,1), we have

    (q_i)_u=J_i v_i=sum_k (k-i)Delta_ik
             =sum_(k!=i) |k-i| |Delta_ik|,
    (q_i)_s=J_i w=-sum_k Delta_ik.

Because every nonzero integer |k-i| is at least 1,

    (q_i)_u >= sum_k |Delta_ik| >= |(q_i)_s|.

The scalar gradient cone {A>=|B|} is closed and convex. Therefore scalar
mollification preserves this inequality locally. If two interior points
are joined by a segment with du>=|ds|, the directional derivative of the
mollified function along the segment is nonnegative, so the function
value cannot decrease. Let the mollification radius tend to zero. For
boundary endpoints first replace both points x,y by
(1-epsilon)x+epsilon x_c, (1-epsilon)y+epsilon x_c for one interior x_c;
their difference remains in the same cone. Passing epsilon to zero proves
the segment comparison on the closed transformed polygon.

Only one scalar coordinate is mollified in this argument. We have NOT
mollified the vector profile while asserting that its nonconvex minor
constraint is preserved.

## 3. Entire generic level sets are proper arcs

Fix i and write ell_i(x)=det(v_i,x)=s. Its extreme values on Omega are

    s_i^- = sum_(j<i) (j-i),
    s_i^+ = sum_(j>i) (j-i).

The extreme fibers are precisely the two color-i sides. Along each of
those sides, in increasing u, q_i rises linearly from 0 to 1.

For s strictly between the extremes, the fiber of the convex transformed
polygon is an interval [l(s),r(s)]. Its left endpoint has boundary height
q_i=0 and its right endpoint has q_i=1. Here is the boundary check. Remove
the two color-i sides from the canonical polygon boundary. The remaining
two chains join their corresponding endpoints, one with h_i identically
0 and one with h_i identically 1. Along each chain ell_i moves strictly
between its two extremes, because the other edge determinants j-i are
nonzero and the boundary directions occur in cyclic order. These are the
two fiber endpoint chains. On the extreme color-i sides, h_i=0 is the
smaller-u endpoint and h_i=1 the larger-u endpoint; the two chains cannot
exchange their left/right order without meeting. This proves the claim,
including the extreme fibers by continuity.

Fix t in (0,1). Along each u-fiber the function is continuous and
nondecreasing by section 2, so its t-level is a nonempty closed interval

    [a_t(s),b_t(s)] subset (l(s),r(s)),

with the evident interpretation at the two extreme fibers. Before
a_t(s) the value is strictly less than t; after b_t(s) it is strictly
greater than t.

Both a_t and b_t are 1-Lipschitz in s. Indeed, if
a_t(s_2)>a_t(s_1)+|s_2-s_1|, choose a point u_2 in fiber 2 strictly
between max(l(s_2),a_t(s_1)+|s_2-s_1|) and a_t(s_2). The cone comparison
from (a_t(s_1),s_1) to (u_2,s_2) gives q_i(u_2,s_2)>=t, contradicting
the definition of a_t(s_2). Exchange the fibers for the reverse bound.
If b_t(s_2)>b_t(s_1)+|s_2-s_1|, choose u_1 in fiber 1 strictly between
b_t(s_1) and min(r(s_1),b_t(s_2)-|s_2-s_1|). The same comparison from
(u_1,s_1) to (b_t(s_2),s_2) contradicts q_i(u_1,s_1)>t. Again exchange
the fibers. The strictly interior choices exist because 0<t<1.

If a_t(s_0)<b_t(s_0) somewhere, s_0 is not an extreme fiber, where the
boundary ramp has a unique t-point. The Lipschitz bounds make the width
positive on an interval of s-values. The whole intervening strip belongs
to h_i=t and has positive two-dimensional area; the coordinate change
has determinant 1. There are at most countably many t with a positive
area level set, because those measurable sets are pairwise disjoint in
a polygon of finite area. For example, levels of area >=1/n form a
finite set for each n.

Outside that countable exceptional set, a_t=b_t everywhere. The entire
level set is consequently

    gamma_(i,t)(s)=a_t(s) v_i+s w,
    s_i^- <=s<=s_i^+.

It is a Lipschitz graph, is injective because ell_i(gamma(s))=s, and
meets the boundary only at its prescribed points on the color-i sides.
Thus it is one proper simple arc; there are no omitted islands, closed
loops, extra boundary contacts, or rank offsets. Different t-levels of
the same scalar coordinate are disjoint.

## 4. Almost every pair of levels has exactly one crossing

Fix i<j and F=(h_i,h_j). On the positively oriented boundary of Omega,
F traces the boundary of the unit square once counterclockwise, allowing
constant pauses on the other color edges. In order, the nonconstant parts
are

    (0,0)->(1,0)->(1,1)->(0,1)->(0,0).

The boundary therefore has winding number 1 about every y in (0,1)^2.

Let N be a measurable null exceptional set outside which h is
differentiable, VDh=I, and all its ordered minors are nonnegative. Its
image under the Lipschitz map F has planar measure zero. On the
differentiability set let C={x:det DF(x)=0}. The area formula for the
locally Lipschitz map F on the open polygon gives

    integral_C |det DF| = integral_(R^2) # (C intersection F^(-1)(y)) dy=0.

It follows that F(C) has planar measure zero. The non-differentiability
null-image assertion also follows directly by covering the null set by
squares and using the Lipschitz bound. The precise analytic import is
Simon's author-posted text, Chapter 2, Theorems 1.2, 1.4 and 3.3; the
source record states the exact open-domain and measure hypotheses.

Choose y in the open square outside F(N union C). It has no boundary
preimage. Every preimage x is an interior differentiability point with
invertible DF(x) of positive determinant. Differentiability at x gives

    F(z)-y=A(z-x)+o(||z-x||),       det A>0.

The least singular value of A is positive. On a sufficiently small
circle or polygon about x, the error is smaller than ||A(z-x)||. Thus x
is isolated in the fiber, and the image of that small boundary has
winding number +1 about y by the straight-line homotopy to A(z-x).
The fiber is a closed subset of compact Omega with no boundary points;
if it were infinite, an accumulation point in the fiber would contradict
this isolation. It is therefore finite.

The outer winding number is the sum of these local winding numbers.
An elementary proof in this precise situation removes disjoint small
polygonal disks around the finitely many preimages. On the remaining
compact polygon with holes, (F-y)/||F-y|| is continuous. Triangulate it
so finely that on each triangle its image is contained in a proper arc
of the circle. Each triangle then has zero winding; summing their
oriented boundaries cancels the interior edges. This proves the claimed
outer-minus-inner winding identity without an openness assumption on F.

Consequently

    1=wind(F on boundary Omega,y)=# F^(-1)(y).

In particular, for almost every pair of levels (t_i,t_j) there is exactly
one intersection of the two ENTIRE level sets, and its ordered minor is
strictly positive. If the two scalar levels also avoid the countable
exceptional sets in section 3, these level sets are simple proper arcs.
Their boundary endpoints alternate, since they lie on the two opposite
color-i and color-j sides in cyclic order. The unique intersection is
therefore a crossing, rather than a tangential contact: one proper arc
separates the disk and the other has its two endpoints on opposite sides.
At the intersection, the above positive local winding and the invertible
derivative give the positive cyclic order of the four sectors in
(h_i,h_j) coordinates.

There is also no local wild-crossing issue. At this point the row
identity gives (q_i)_u>=Delta_ij>0. Insert the 1-Lipschitz graph
u=a_t(s) into the differentiability expansion of q_i. Its slope at the
point exists and equals -(q_i)_s/(q_i)_u. Thus the level arc has a
nonzero tangent in the kernel of Dh_i. The same holds for j, and the
two tangents are distinct because Delta_ij>0. In a small neighborhood
their four branches lie in four separate narrow cones. Together with
the unique-intersection assertion this gives the ordinary tame four-arc
crossing used in the finite plane graph below. No claim that F is a
local homeomorphism on a whole neighborhood follows from differentiability
alone, and none is needed.

Each excluded set has D-dimensional measure zero: in the indicated

The resulting pD arcs have precisely the properties in GR2. Intersections

## 6. The dual is a disk with the full boundary

We give the finite topological argument so that neither an arbitrary
straightening of pseudolines nor an unproved planar dual embedding is
hidden. A finite union of the simple arcs above, with their distinct
proper crossings and endpoints, is a finite embedded planar graph.
The arcs are Lipschitz graphs, and at each crossing their local sectors
have the proper crossing order. Between the finitely many vertices each
edge is a simple arc. The graph is therefore a finite topological plane
graph, with the usual disk/crosscut constructions applicable.

First every complementary region in Omega is a Jordan disk and has a
unique vector of sides of the individual arcs. Add the arcs one at a
time. This is true for the initial disk. Along a new arc every old arc
is crossed at most once, so each old side bit changes at most once.
The sequence of old side vectors along the new arc cannot repeat: any
nonempty portion between two occurrences would change some old bit once
and could not change it back. By induction an old region has a unique
old side vector, so it is visited at most once. Each nonempty visit is
one proper crosscut of that Jordan disk, splitting it into two Jordan
disks with different new side bits. Unvisited disks remain disks. This
also proves uniqueness of the new full side vectors. No triple crossing
or old vertex is encountered during this construction.

Give each region its rank vector

    a_i = # {k : (k+eta_i)/p < h_i(x)}

for any x in that region. It is constant on the region, belongs to
{0,...,p}^D, and determines all individual side bits since the levels of
one color are ordered. Thus distinct regions have distinct rank vectors.
Across an arc of color i the rank changes by exactly e_i.

The boundary circle minus its 2Dp ports consists of 2Dp intervals.
Their region ranks follow exactly

    0 -> p e_1 -> p(e_1+e_2) -> ... -> p(1,...,1)
      -> p(0,1,...,1) -> ... -> 0,

with all unit intermediate steps included. For D>=2 these 2Dp boundary
rank vectors are distinct, except the closing repetition of the initial
one. A region consequently cannot touch two different boundary intervals.
Nor can an additional region touch the boundary at an isolated point:
near a port only its single terminating arc is present, and near every
other boundary point no arc ends. The two port-adjacent regions each
meet their corresponding interval. Hence every boundary region meets
exactly one boundary interval.

Here is an explicit relative-dual construction. Regard the boundary
intervals as curved graph edges and the ports as their vertices; polygon
corners of degree two are absorbed into those edges. Include the outside
disk to get a cellular graph on the sphere. Its internal vertices are
the crossings, of degree four; its boundary vertices are the ports, of
degree three. The Jordan-region property permits the usual planar dual
construction by joining region centers through edge midpoints inside
each region (equivalently, using the barycentric subdivision). Each
crossing gives a quadrilateral dual face. Each port gives a triangular
dual face containing the outside dual vertex O.

Every boundary interval belongs to a different inside region. The closed
star of O is thus the cone over the simple cycle of these 2Dp distinct
region vertices: its triangles are exactly the port triangles in their
cyclic order. It is a closed disk. Remove its open star from the dual
sphere. What remains is a quadrangulated closed disk K whose boundary
cycle has those 2Dp region vertices. There are no uncrossed arcs forming
extra boundary-to-boundary dual edges: every arc crosses the p arcs in
each other color, and D>=2. This construction in particular rules out a
pinched dual boundary or a separate hidden exterior component.

## 7. Positive quadrilaterals give an embedded tiling

At the unique crossing x_0 of levels (i,k) and (j,l), i<j, its four
dual vertices have ranks

    a,  a+e_i,  a+e_i+e_j,  a+e_j

in positive cyclic order. Here a_i=k, a_j=l, and the other coordinates
are the numbers of their levels below h at x_0. The positive local
winding from section 4 supplies this cyclic order; it is not a choice
made separately on each face.

Map every dual vertex with rank a to V a. Map its edges linearly and
map each quadrilateral affinely as

    (s,t) -> V a+s v_i+t v_j,   0<=s,t<=1.

These definitions agree along glued edges. Every face is a nondegenerate
positively oriented unit parallelogram because det(v_i,v_j)=j-i>0.
The full boundary cycle maps homeomorphically, with its unit subdivisions,
onto the positively oriented boundary of Z_p.

One can verify global degree one by winding directly. For any physical
point z not on the finite union of image edges, sum the winding numbers
of all oriented face boundaries. All internal edges cancel, leaving
the winding of the outer boundary. Each positively oriented image
parallelogram contributes 1 in its interior and 0 outside. Therefore

    sum_faces 1_(interior image face)(z) = 1_(interior Z_p)(z).

So the image face interiors are disjoint, none lies outside Z_p, and
their closures cover Z_p. This is a degree-one conclusion, not merely a
positive Jacobian assertion on individual faces.

The geometry is edge-to-edge. Every face vertex is integral and every
tile edge has horizontal displacement +1 or -1. An integral vertex
cannot lie strictly inside such an edge. Two parallel tile edges with
a nontrivial overlap must have the same consecutive-integer horizontal
projection, and hence coincide completely. Nonparallel interior edge
crossings would force positive-area overlap of the two convex tiles;
that has just been excluded. Thus two different image tiles intersect
only in a whole edge, a common vertex, or the empty set.

For completeness the map of K has no additional collapsed identifications.
Two different internal dual edges cannot have the same geometric image:
each already has its two incident faces on opposite sides, and a further
incident face would force an interior overlap. An internal edge cannot
map to a boundary edge because it has faces on both sides. The boundary
map is already injective. At an internal vertex, the positive corner
angles of its cyclic incident faces sum to 2 pi times a positive integer.
Any winding above one, or any second internal vertex mapped to the same
point, would again force overlapping face interiors in a sufficiently
small sector. Such a vertex also cannot map to the boundary, where its
full positive angular neighborhood would leave Z_p. Hence vertex stars
are embedded, distinct vertices cannot be identified, and the entire
disk map is a homeomorphism onto Z_p.

We have therefore obtained an actual full tiling T_p. Its region ranks
give its canonical lift, with origin rank 0; there is no unresolved
height monodromy. Every pair of colors has p^2 arc crossings, so it has
p^2 tiles of that type. The accepted integer-deformation dictionary
identifies this tiling with an element counted by the marked B_D(p).
This argument never asserts that the pseudolines can all be straightened
to one multigrid.

## 8. Quantitative comparison of the profiles (GR3)

Consider the normalized image of a tile associated to the original
crossing x_0 in Omega. At all four dual vertices a/p of that tile,

    ||a/p-h(x_0)||_infinity <=1/p.

For a crossing color, its threshold (k+eta_i)/p lies strictly between
the adjacent heights k/p and (k+1)/p. For a noncrossing color, the value
h_i(x_0) is between the two thresholds surrounding its count a_i;
the same <=1/p estimate holds, including counts 0 and p. No triple
threshold occurs by section 5.

The canonical lift is affine on the image tile, so the same estimate
holds for its interpolated normalized height h_(T_p)(y) at every point
y of that normalized tile. Since V h_(T_p)(y)=y and Vh(x_0)=x_0,

    ||y-x_0||_2 <= M_D/p.

By the coordinate D-Lipschitz bound in section 2,

    ||h_(T_p)(y)-h(y)||_infinity
       <=1/p+D ||y-x_0||_2 <=(1+D M_D)/p.

All of Omega is covered by these tiles, and continuity includes their
edges and the boundary. This proves GR3 with one uniform constant,
independent of the profile, its flat regions, its number of phases,
and p. It is a geometric approximation rate for one profile only.

## 9. Rational finite PL data and exact admissible-profile description

Suppose h is given on a finite rational triangulation, with V_0 vertices,
E_0 edges and F_0 triangles, rational coordinate values, and affine
interpolation. The hypotheses can be checked by finitely many exact
rational equalities and inequalities: continuity on shared edges,
the full boundary values, range at vertices, Vh=x on each triangle,
and the ordered minors of every constant triangle gradient.

There is a finite larger bad-phase set avoiding all analytic exceptions:

* Scalar levels: avoid every value of h_i at a triangulation vertex.
* Pair levels: avoid the image under (h_i,h_j) of every triangulation
  edge and every rank-deficient triangle. Each such image is contained
  in a proper rational affine line in R^2; for a point choose any
  proper rational line through it.
* Triple levels: avoid the image of every triangle under (h_i,h_j,h_k).
  Each lies in a proper rational affine plane in R^3; lower-dimensional
  images can be included in any such plane.

Substituting t_(i,k)=(k+eta_i)/p makes every avoidance a proper rational
affine hyperplane in the phase space R^D. The total number H is bounded by

    H <= D V_0 p
         + binom(D,2)(E_0+F_0)p^2
         + binom(D,3) F_0 p^3.

This is an upper bound; redundant, empty or repeated exclusions may be
kept. A rank-deficient triangle image is covered by one line, and a
pair edge image by one line, so no hidden continuum of conditions occurs.

Use the moment curve eta_i=t^i. Every proper affine hyperplane restricts
to a nonzero polynomial c_0+sum_(i=1)^D c_i t^i of degree at most D.
It therefore excludes at most D real values of t. Among the DH+1 distinct
rational candidates

    t=j/(DH+2),   j=1,...,DH+1,

at least one avoids all H hyperplanes. Test them exactly and take the
first successful one. (For H=0 the same rule gives t=1/2.) This is a
finite rational phase algorithm, not an appeal to the existence of a
rational point outside an arbitrary null set.

For these phases every scalar level crosses the triangulation in a
finite polygonal arc; every pair intersection is in a triangle interior
with a positive invertible pair gradient; no triple intersection occurs.
One may find the unique pair intersection by solving the two affine
equations in each triangle and then output the parallelogram with the
rank origin described in section 7. All calculations are rational and
finite. This describes a construction with its necessary finite output;
no mathematical program, enumeration run or certificate is used here.

Finally let X_D be the uniform closure of normalized profiles of actual
full balanced tilings, allowing unbounded integer side contents. Such
profiles have common boundary h_b, lie in [0,1]^D, satisfy Vh=x and the
minor inequalities, and have the uniform D-Lipschitz bound. The minor
constraint passes to uniform limits: in distributions,

    det(grad f,grad g)
       =partial_1(f partial_2 g)-partial_2(f partial_1 g).

Uniform convergence of f and weak-* convergence of the bounded
derivatives of g pass this identity to the limit. To see the product
step explicitly, against a smooth compactly supported test function
split (f_n-f) partial_j g_n from f(partial_j g_n-partial_j g); the first
term tends to zero by uniform convergence and the derivative bound,
and the second by weak-* convergence with the L^1 test coefficient f.
Each approximating minor is nonnegative, so the limit distribution is
nonnegative and its almost-everywhere density is nonnegative.
All the full admissibility conditions therefore hold on X_D.

Conversely GR3 constructs h_(T_p) converging uniformly to any admissible
full h along EVERY integer p. Hence X_D equals exactly this admissible
class. Integer subdivision of an existing tiling also shows that taking
limits with side content tending to infinity, rather than merely closure
of their union, gives the same set. The rational PL profiles produced by
actual tilings are consequently dense in this class. This characterization
does not make the class convex; it retains every pointwise minor constraint.
