# Failed routes, repaired inferences and remaining upper interface

2026-10-17 UTC. **Candidate proof - unverified.** These are genuine
reasoning attempts and their outcomes, not mathematical test results.
No enumeration, CAS, scan or author rerun was performed.

## Direct rank-sampling/Shearer shortcut

Restrict a large parallel-bundle arrangement to small subsets of its
rank tracks and try to bound the full entropy by the entropy of one
small balanced count. The obvious invariant is a triple-order/sign
record. Uniformly sampling p ranks of each color retains a given
three-color triple with probability(p/N)^3, so the elementary Shearer
bound has the wrong cubic scaling. It does not produce the required
quadratic finite-side upper rate. Replacing it by pair information
without proving recovery loses the order constraints. No improved
rank-sampling theorem was established.

## Free-boundary grid or finite LP shortcut

Cut a large tiling into fixed physical squares or selected rank faces.
A local code does yield finite upper bounds in terms of patch counts.
However independently maximizing those counts can change the boundary
flux or choose incompatible profiles. An aggregate tile-frequency LP
has necessary constraints, not a proved lower realization theorem.
This would reintroduce arbitrary-Q decoration sufficiency, blocked by
the accepted exact small lifting counterexamples. No equality with the
balanced fixed-boundary entropy, convergent upper sequence or numerical
value was inferred. It remains a possible upper-bound research route
only after proving both relevant entropy inequalities.

## Dyadic mean gradients and convexification

An early attempt used mean gradients directly and would have silently
assumed their minor inequalities survive averaging. The accepted
fixed-frame nonconvex example excludes that inference. The candidate
uses the L2-best affine fit only to preserve VJ=I, then proves an
approximate minor bound by the exact boundary Green formula, and finally
uses a finite-dimensional near-K coverage test. It does not convexify K.

## L2 control is not buffered sup control

A scalar L2 affine approximation alone does not meet whole-buffer
recovery hypotheses. The best-fit affine slope must first be bounded.
The explicit slope formula gives norm<=3G, so the difference is
Lipschitz; the small-ball argument then gives a sup estimate with
the tau^4 bad-area bound. Boundary/corner maxima are included.

## A parameter-order defect found during construction

An early draft suggested shrinking finite catalogue balls after selecting
packing templates. Shrinking an already selected finite cover can destroy
coverage. Before freeze this was corrected: use uniform boundary shape
bounds to choose the boundary packing span BEFORE the boundary cover;
after rho, choose strict frames and packing spans BEFORE its finite
center-net coverage; then choose the near-K test and flatness tolerance.
The rational periods are last among these local choices. The frozen
candidate states this corrected noncircular order. No earlier frozen
candidate or review was edited.

## A polynomial error-map bound corrected before freeze

The first draft's boundary polynomial bound was linear in H, which
was too small to transparently dominate the full I-J*W error map.
It was replaced before freeze by B=2^10 D^8(H+1)^2; from
||A||<=D^2H, det A>=1/D, the required row/inverse/error bounds
fit inside this larger explicit bound. This is a constructor correction,
not a peer review finding or a change to an older packet.

The proposed theorem supplies max(0,a_D(p)-k_D) from above, hence
k_D>=a_D(p)-epsilon. It does NOT give k_D<=a_D(p)+epsilon. The
accepted V4 abstract sequence even has exact finite square replication
and zero positive excess while its limit is noncomputable. Therefore
the new uniform finite-M strengthening does not reverse the inequality.
The separate upper hypothesis R of V3 remains open, as do any actual
numerical evaluation, practical complexity/rate and full annual answer.
