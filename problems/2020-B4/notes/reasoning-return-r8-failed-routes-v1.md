## 1. A field norm is still not an integral phase

Hasse's theorem initially looked promising because all local tests
for q are manageable. R8A does prove a global z with z*c(z)=q.
That alone does NOT give an actual subset. The ideal of z may have
negative exponents on one member of a conjugate pair, and its inverse
Fourier coefficients have not become integral. Passing from field
norms to integral norms is exactly the arithmetic step in question.
The candidate explicitly adds a principal balanced INTEGRAL ideal
and then proves the correcting-unit assertion on its stated domain.

The old R7D balanced nonprincipal ideal at q5 remains a concrete
barrier: field-norm solubility at those parameters cannot make that
particular ideal principal. Nor does failure of one such ideal exclude
all others or the known actual order-five examples.

## 2. Positivity and a norm-one kernel cannot give unit correction

The ratio epsilon=N(gamma)/q is a totally positive unit, and the
accepted norm-one kernel in F is+/-1. Neither statement says epsilon
is a unit norm. The kernel only describes two successful generators
after existence; it does not describe the norm map's image.

The genuine replacement is the CLASSICAL quotient of ambiguous by
strongly ambiguous classes. Its full quadratic exact sequence is
proved in the main, with both kernel directions and all representative
choices. It is a finite2-primary obstruction. Under odd h(F) it
vanishes; without that or another proof of Gamma=0, an individual
o(a) must still be tested. No universal unit-norm assertion survives.

In this exact field every unit is real, so unit norms are squares.
This does NOT make all totally positive real units squares. The
specific epsilon is first proved a FIELD norm via R8A, and only
then the odd-class obstruction theorem supplies its unit square root.

## 5. Full exponent divisibility does not control every intermediate ideal

A scratch strengthening of the exponent test would have said that
exp C dividing every e_ell makes EVERY balanced ideal principal.
That is not justified: intermediate a_i in0..e_ell need not be
multiples of exp C. The candidate asserts only that the chosen
EXTREME ideal with all a_i=0 is principal. That is enough for the
sufficient existence statement. It does not classify every ideal.

The long-interval test instead constructs each actual half residue
2a_i=e_i modulo ord(delta_i) in its permitted range and then uses
oddness to remove2-torsion. Neither test is advertised as necessary;
only the complete class-group interval condition is an iff on the
stated odd-class-number domain.
