# Deep literature checkpoint: 2020 B4(b)

Status: literature investigation and proposed directions, not a full proof. Verified 2026-10-16 UTC. Requested gpt-6.1-sol/max; effective settings unavailable. Only new files bearing this prefix are edited.

The current accepted partial classification was read before searching. For n>=2 put q=n−1, v=q²+q+1, k=q+1. The open contest classification is exactly cyclic planar difference-set existence with these parameters, together with the small q=1 boundary. Published bounded exclusions and a density theorem do not prove the unrestricted cyclic prime-power conjecture. No verified later universal classification was found in this pass.

## Findings that add something beyond the basic references

**A sharper residue filter.** Peluse, [*An asymptotic version of the prime power conjecture for perfect difference sets*, arXiv:2003.04929v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.04929v2), Lemma2.2, p3, assumes v=q²+q+1 is prime and v≡1 mod4. Every prime dividing q must then be a quartic residue modulo v. The proof uses Hall's multiplier result and the impossibility of −1 being a multiplier. This is a concrete additional test where Mann's quadratic-residue condition can be vacuous. Theorem1.2 is asymptotic, counting orders up to N by ~N/logN; it leaves a possible sparse exceptional set and supplies no universal cutoff. The full PDF was cached, and the lemma's hypotheses and proof were read and visually checked.

A slightly stronger **inference from that proof**, not a new theorem claim: when v is prime, each known numerical multiplier must have odd order, since the unique involution of F_v^* is −1. Thus one can test the entire 2-part of the order, beyond just a fourth-power test. For composite v, an even-order projection at one prime factor need not produce global −1, so that shortcut cannot be used componentwise without additional proof. In general, an exact test whether −1 lies in the subgroup generated by prime divisors of q is sound under the same multiplier input. The existing q=2435 calculation already excludes that order by another multiplier argument; repeating it would add no classification.

**Collision certificates beyond orbit sizes.** Baumert–Gordon's [*On the existence of cyclic difference sets with small parameters*](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0304502), Theorem5.1, and Gordon's [*On difference sets with small λ*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.07292), arXiv Theorem6, state the Evans–Mann/Lander test: if numerical multipliers t1,t2,t3,t4 satisfy t1−t2≡t3−t4 modulo v, then v divides lcm(t1−t2,t1−t3). The first paper explains its use in excluding its605 residual planar orders. An exact collision search can produce a four-integer certificate even when the multiplier subgroup size is <=k and an orbit-size test survives. Gordon's separate contracted-multiplier bound (arXiv Theorem5; numbering differs in publication) bounds a group already proved to act on a quotient; it does not certify arbitrary new multipliers.

**What field descent actually gives.** Leung–Schmidt, [*The field descent method*](https://personal.ntu.edu.sg/bernhard/Publications/pub/field.pdf), author version, Theorem4.3, p18: for G=A×H with coprime factor orders, a difference set of order q with gcd(q,|H|)=1 has an exponent bound involving F=gcd(expA,F(expG,q)); if A is cyclic, 4q φ(F)<=|H|F². Taking A=C_v,H=1 yields 4q φ(F(v,q))<=F(v,q)². The definition2.6 ensures rad(v)|F(v,q)|v. Consequently squarefree v gives F=v and this specialization is generally weak. Field descent can constrain repeated-prime conductor factors; it cannot be advertised as a universal small-conductor or prime-power-order theorem. Its group-ring descent output has integer coefficients and must not silently be treated as a 0/1 difference set.

Leung–Ma–Schmidt, [*A Multiplier Theorem*](https://personal.ntu.edu.sg/bernhard/Publications/pub/multipliers.pdf), Theorem1.4, pp3–4, replaces the usual n1>λ hypothesis by a recursive coprimality test. The assumptions include n1|q, gcd(v,n1)=1, and for every prime u|n1 a congruence t≡u^fu mod expG; the additional condition is gcd(v,M(q/n1,floor(k/n1)))=1. The recursion can depend on the order of its prime choices. For λ=1, prime divisors of q are already multipliers. A proposed improvement must therefore show an actual new t or stronger quotient information, not merely reprove the Hall multipliers. This source is a good targeted tool, not an automatic solution of the cyclic conjecture.

## Square descent and stronger-looking theorems with extra hypotheses

The accepted square-descent argument already sends a cyclic planar order q=a² to a smaller cyclic planar order a. This immediately propagates an exclusion through iterated squaring, but it does not address nonsquare non-prime-powers. Calling every group-ring projection a descended difference set would be an invalid extension.

Huang–Schmidt, [*Uniqueness of Some Cyclic Projective Planes*](https://personal.ntu.edu.sg/bernhard/Publications/pub/Singer.pdf), author version dated2008, published DCC50 (2009),253–266, explicitly separates prime-power **existence** from Desarguesian **uniqueness**. Its section3 assumes q is a prime power and studies planes of order q². Results6–10 and Lemmas12–15 convert a square-order plane into a prescribed subplane plus an equivariant function f_D with exact distinctness constraints. Its listed uniqueness orders include all orders<41 and121,125,128,169,256,1024. This could guide efficient finite extension tests after a valid descent, but its prime-power starting assumption cannot be used to prove every square order is a prime power.

Blokhuis–Jungnickel–Schmidt, [*Proof of the prime power conjecture for projective planes of order n with abelian collineation groups of order n²*](https://personal.ntu.edu.sg/bernhard/Publications/pub/blojunew.pdf), PAMS130 (2002),1473–1476, has a different group hypothesis. A contest solution supplies a regular cyclic group of order n²+n+1, not an abelian collineation group of order n². No implication between those hypotheses was established. The PDF's text encoding is damaged; the title and abstract were verified from a rendering. No detailed proof import is made.

## Recent adjacent work and novelty risks

Alexeev–Mixon, [*Forbidden Sidon subsets of perfect difference sets, featuring a human-assisted proof*, arXiv:2510.19804v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.19804v2), Theorems8–9, gives forbidden finite subsets. Theorem8 rules out {1,2,4,8} for **prime** order, whereas Theorem9 rules out {1,2,4,8,13} for every finite modulus. The first proof's parity mechanism is visible in section3; prime order is not interchangeable with prime-power order. These are configuration restrictions, not a solution of existence by order. The source also recovers Hall's1947 counterexample, a warning that a plausible computational novelty may have an old structural proof.

Tong Niu's [arXiv:2604.25214](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.25214), 2026-04-28, explicitly presents bounded Singer-orbit and small-modulus searches and says a complete proof remains open. Only its metadata/abstract were checked here, so no mathematical theorem is imported. In particular, testing only Singer affine orbits is not exhaustive over arbitrary cyclic planes unless an applicable uniqueness theorem is supplied. The cyclic prime-power conjecture alone asserts allowed orders, not uniqueness of cyclic planes at those orders. This distinction blocks one tempting extrapolation from finite data.

## Bounded research actions supported by the sources

1. Add the prime-v odd-order/quartic filter to a future certificate generator, recording its prime-modulus hypothesis. Run it only as a cross-check or on orders outside already classified ranges; it adds no unrestricted proof.
2. For a small explicit residual family, benchmark an exact multiplier-difference collision hash before a run with a fixed timeout, PID and checkpoints. Each rejection should retain t1,t2,t3,t4 and the failed divisibility, giving a short independently checkable certificate.
3. Prioritize nonsquare q with repeated-prime v factors if trying field descent. Squarefree conductors are a predictable weak case of the displayed bound.
4. For square orders, first apply the valid smaller-order existence obstruction. Use the Huang–Schmidt function model only after its additional hypotheses are met.
