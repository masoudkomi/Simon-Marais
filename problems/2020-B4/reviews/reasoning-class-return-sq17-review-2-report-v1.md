## 1. Full reconstruction of SQ17A

Let m be odd, at least3, with at least TWO distinct prime divisors.
Set K=Q(zeta_m), E=K^c, epsilon=1-zeta_m. This excludes prime powers,
including odd squares of primes; the word composite alone would be too
weak. No squarefree hypothesis is needed.

For l prime, the geometric-series formula gives Phi_(l^a)(1)=l.
The identity (X^m-1)/(X-1)=product_(d|m,d>1)Phi_d(X) gives at X1

    m = product_(d|m,d>1) Phi_d(1).

For non-prime-power m, all its prime-power divisors are proper.
Strong induction makes all proper non-prime-power factors equal1,
and the prime-power factors contribute
product_(l|m) l^v_l(m)=m. Thus Phi_m(1)=1. This reasoning covers
repeated conductor primes and avoids any assumption about finite
examples. The conjugates of epsilon are algebraic integers with
product1, so their product excluding epsilon is its integral inverse.
Therefore epsilon is an actual integral UNIT, not merely a nonzero
integer or a field element with rational norm.

Direct cancellation has the important MINUS sign:

    epsilon/c(epsilon)
      = (1-zeta_m)/(1-zeta_m^-1) = -zeta_m.

Both epsilon and its inverse are integral. Hence
u0=epsilon*c(epsilon) and eta=u0^-1 are real units. Each real embedding
of E extends to an embedding of K; its image of u0 is the squared
modulus of a nonzero image of epsilon. Thus u0 and eta are totally
positive at EVERY real embedding, not just the displayed one.

Suppose eta=w² in E. Since w² and w^-2 are integral, w and w^-1 are
integral: integrality over O_E and integral closedness, or transitivity
over Z, gives this directly. Thus even an initially fractional square
root would be a real unit. Now xi=epsilon*w is an integral unit with
xi*c(xi)=1. K is abelian, so each embedding commutes with c, and all
conjugates of xi have modulus1.

The bounded-polynomial argument suffices for the invoked Kronecker
step. For every positive a, xi^a has degree at most D=[K:Q]. Each
coefficient of its monic minimal polynomial is an integer bounded in
absolute value by a binomial coefficient depending only on D. There
are finitely many such polynomials and finitely many roots of them.
Two positive powers coincide; xi is nonzero and hence a root of unity.
This does not assume its norm1 alone, without all conjugate bounds.

For a root of unity c(xi)=xi^-1, whereas w is real. It follows that

    xi² = xi/c(xi) = epsilon/c(epsilon) = -zeta_m.

As m is odd, (xi^m)²=-1. Thus K would contain i. But adjoining i to
Q(zeta_m) gives Q(zeta_(4m)), since gcd(4,m)=1; their degrees are
phi(m) and phi(4m)=2phi(m). This contradiction proves eta is not a
square in E. Its inverse u0 is likewise not a square there. Every
sign, embedding, integrality and oddness hypothesis used here matches
the author's statement. In particular the argument is not valid by
this reasoning at even m, which is outside SQ17A.

    U --> mu/mu², u |--> [u/c(u)]
