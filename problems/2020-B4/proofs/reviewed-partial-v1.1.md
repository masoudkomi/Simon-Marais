# Reviewed cyclic difference-set reductions

**Proved, independently reviewed (partial results)**, 2026-10-16 UTC.
This accepts P1–P6 and the separately attributed square-descent application
in the complete [frozen argument](partial-v1.md), SHA-256
`f53945e49bd626847e52a61f7e39e96defd2ae44900f9d235c2c14e720f41525`.
Its original candidate labels are historical. No mathematical argument
has been changed. The [acceptance record](../reviews/acceptance.md)
identifies both blind full reconstructions and the source limitations.

For the official integer domain n≥2, put q=n−1 and v=q²+q+1. The
accepted results are the exact equivalence with a cyclic (v,q+1,1)
difference set, the n=2 boundary, and, for q≥2, the correspondence
with planes admitting a cyclic regular group **of collineations**.
The last qualification applies throughout P2, including its converse.
Arbitrary point permutations do not suffice.

The other accepted arguments give a common sum-zero multiplier
normalization, quotient occupancy and character identities, the
necessary norm condition q=x²−xy+y² when q≡1 modulo 3, and arithmetic
multiplier-orbit sizes with a bounded subset-sum necessary test.
In particular no n≡11 modulo 12 is taut. Passing these necessary
conditions supplies no existence result.

The published square-descent and Wilbrink statements exclude q=2304,
hence n=2305: descent would give a cyclic plane of order 48, whereas
v₃(48)=1 and 48≠3. Their statements and application hypotheses were
checked in Gordon's 1994 paper; the original imported proofs were not
reconstructed. This is an attributed application, not a new theorem.

The [source clarification](../notes/reviewed-source-clarifications-v1.md)
fixes preprint versus publisher numbering for ancillary computational
theorems and explains why zero orbit counts must be allowed. Neither
requires a mathematical repair to the frozen argument or its code.

The unrestricted cyclic Prime Power Conjecture and thus the complete
B4(b) classification remain unresolved here. No novelty is claimed.
Independent finite checks supplement the written proofs; the large
published verification range has not been rerun or independently certified.
