## R5A. Exact coset criterion and a complete image polynomial

This paragraph is standalone. Let \(m\ge3\) be odd,
\(\epsilon\in\{0,1\}\), and suppose

\[
p=m^2+(2\epsilon-1)m+1\text{ is prime},\quad
L=m+2\epsilon-1,\quad p-1=mL.
\]

Let \(H\le\mathbb F_p^\times\) have order \(m\), and put
\(D_0=H,D_1=H\cup\{0\}\). Since m is odd, \(-1\notin H\).
For each \(h\in H\setminus\{1\}\), the ordered pairs
\((bh,b)\), \(b\in H\), contribute each member of the multiplicative
coset \((h-1)H\) exactly once as a difference. These pairs, over
all h, are exactly the off-diagonal pairs from H. When epsilon=1,
the remaining off-diagonal pairs contribute H and -H, each once.
Consequently D-epsilon is planar if and only if

\[
h\longmapsto(h-1)H                                \tag{A1}
\]

is bijective from \(H\setminus\{1\}\) onto ALL L cosets when
epsilon=0, or onto all cosets EXCEPT H and -H when epsilon=1.
The cardinalities are respectively m-1=L and m-1=L-2. This is an
integer count criterion; no congruence-to-count lifting is omitted.

The homomorphism \(x\mapsto x^m\) identifies the coset quotient with
\(\mu_L\subset\mathbb F_p^\times\), since its kernel has exactly
m elements. Thus the criterion is also the bijectivity of

\[
\delta(h)=(h-1)^m
\]

onto \(\mu_L\) or \(\mu_L\setminus\{1,-1\}\), respectively.
The two omitted cosets are distinct, and their images are 1 and -1
because m is odd. Equivalently, in \(\mathbb F_p[X]\),

\[
\prod_{h\in H\setminus\{1\}}(X-(h-1)^m)=
\begin{cases}
X^{m-1}-1,&\epsilon=0,\\[2mm]
\dfrac{X^{m+1}-1}{X^2-1}
 =1+X^2+\cdots+X^{m-1},&\epsilon=1.
\end{cases}                                         \tag{A2}
\]

This is an iff: the right sides have distinct roots, since
\(L<p\). Equality of the monic polynomials therefore supplies the
full bijection, including absence of the omitted images. The quotient
in A2 is a polynomial, not an evaluation at a singular denominator.

Taking products gives a short Hall-free reconstruction of 3|m.
The derivative of \(X^m-1\) at 1 gives
\(\prod_{h\ne1}(1-h)=m\). Since m-1 is even,
\(\prod_{h\ne1}(h-1)=m\). The product of all L-th roots is -1
because L is even; after deleting 1,-1 it is 1. Therefore

\[
m^m=-1\quad(\epsilon=0),\qquad
m^m=1\quad(\epsilon=1)\quad\text{in }\mathbb F_p.     \tag{A3}
\]

For epsilon=0, \(m^2-m+1=0\) gives \(m^3=-1\), so m has
order exactly six: it is neither 1 nor -1, and p is odd. Thus
m is divisible by three (indeed m is odd and m=3 modulo6).
For epsilon=1, \(m^2+m+1=0\) gives order exactly three for m;
m is not 1 modulo p since m>=3 and m<p. A3 then again forces
3|m. This matches, and does not strengthen, accepted R4D's congruence.
The m=3,epsilon=0,L=2 boundary is included throughout.

## R5B. Classical multiplier bound and perfect-power restrictions

### B1. Prime Phi3 values and perfect powers

If \(q=a^d\), \(a\ge2,d\ge1\), and \(q^2+q+1\) is prime,
then d is a power of three. Write \(d=3^s u\), \(3\nmid u\),
and put \(x=a^{3^s}\). Modulo \(x^2+x+1\) one has \(x^3=1\).
Whether u is 1 or 2 modulo3,
\(x^{2u}+x^u+1=0\) modulo \(x^2+x+1\). If u>1, this is a
proper divisor: \(1<x^2+x+1<x^{2u}+x^u+1\). Primality forces
u=1. This includes arbitrary integer perfect powers, not only prime
bases. In particular a saturated q cannot be a square.

Hence, if the actual saturated q is a prime power \(r^e\), the
even row has \(q=2^{3^s}\), and the odd row has
\(q=3^{3^s}\). Indeed the even prime base is 2; in the odd row
accepted \(3\mid q\) forces the prime base 3. This is only a
necessary family restriction, not an assertion that the corresponding
Phi3 values are prime or that the saturated difference sets exist.

### B2. A complete prime-degree Desarguesian normalizer argument

Suppose an ACTUAL cyclic plane from D has order \(q=r^e\) and is
Desarguesian, and suppose \(p=q^2+q+1\) is prime. In this paragraph
D need not satisfy S0. We claim

\[
M=\langle r\rangle\pmod p,\qquad |M|=3e.             \tag{B1}
\]

Use the standard collineation theorem for the CLASSICAL plane only:
\(\operatorname{Aut}(\mathrm{PG}(2,q))=\mathrm{P\Gamma L}_3(q)\).
Its exact formulation was checked in Thas--Zagier2008, p174; the
fundamental projective-geometry theorem is an attributed classical
input, not newly proved here. The normalizer calculation that follows
is complete and does not import a theorem about arbitrary cyclic planes.

Let C be the order-p translation group coming from the cyclic labels.
Projection from P-Gamma-L to the field automorphism group of order e
annihilates C: p>e, and a homomorphism from a group of prime order p
to a group of order e must be trivial. Thus C lies in PGL. Choose a
matrix \(A\in\mathrm{GL}_3(q)\) whose projective class generates C.
A has no nonzero proper invariant subspace. Such a subspace would
give a nonempty C-invariant set of at most q+1 projective points;
every C-orbit has size p, and q+1<p. Consequently its cubic
characteristic polynomial is irreducible. The algebra

\[
E=\mathbb F_q[A]\cong\mathbb F_{q^3}
\]

is a field, and the underlying vector space is one-dimensional over E.
Here \(p>e\) follows, for example, from q>=2^e>=e+1.

A numerical multiplier t induces the collineation x->tx on the cyclic
labels, with lines mapped to lines since tD is a translate of D.
It normalizes C and acts on its generator by exponent t. Represent
this collineation by an invertible semilinear map B. Then

\[
BAB^{-1}=cA^t\qquad(c\in\mathbb F_q^\times).          \tag{B2}
\]

Conjugation by B preserves the field E. It sends scalar multiplication
by a in Fq to scalar multiplication by its field automorphism image,
and it sends A to cA^t. Moreover Fq[A^t]=E: the projective class
of A^t is again a generator of C and the same invariant-subspace
argument makes A^t irreducible. Hence conjugation restricts to an
Fr-automorphism of E. Every such automorphism is a power of the
r-Frobenius, of order dividing 3e. Applying it to A and passing to
projective classes in B2 proves \(t\equiv r^j\pmod p\) for some j.
Thus \(M\subset\langle r\rangle\).

Conversely Hall's already accepted multiplier theorem gives r in M,
because r divides q. Finally the order of r modulo p is exactly 3e.
It divides 3e since p divides r^(3e)-1. A proper divisor d of 3e
is at most 3e/2<2e, so
\(0<r^d-1<r^{2e}+r^e+1=p\), which prevents p dividing r^d-1.
This proves B1, including e=1. No choice of scalar matrix lift or
semilinear representative affects the projective exponent argument.

## R5C. Pair sums and the punctured subgroup

Every subset of an actual planar D has unique UNORDERED pair sums,
including repeated entries. Indeed if a+b=c+d and a!=c, then
a-c=d-b is a nonzero ordered difference; uniqueness forces a=d,c=b.
If a=c, then b=d. This proves the assertion without excluding the
case a=b or c=d.

Let H be either actual subgroup in S1, and let m=|H|. For z!=0
write R(z)=#{(a,b) in H^2:a+b=z}. It is at most two, and it equals
one precisely when z belongs to 2H. The latter case has its unique
diagonal representation and cannot also have an off-diagonal one.
All other values are zero or two. Also R(0)=0 since -1 is not in H.
Choose multiplicative cosets C_j=g^jH; then

\[
a_{ij}=\#\{x\in C_i:1+x\in C_j\},\qquad
R(z)=a_{0j}\quad(z\in C_j).                         \tag{C1}
\]

For the second identity, h=a/b gives the bijection between a+b=z
and h in H with 1+h in zH; its inverse is
\(b=z/(1+h),a=bh\). The denominator is nonzero because -1 is
not in H. Thus the FIRST ROW of the cyclotomic matrix has one entry
one, at coset 2H, exactly (m-1)/2 entries two and all others zero.
Its row sum is m. This gives a direct combinatorial explanation of
the prime-field lambda-one 0-or-2 condition in Sun2025,
Proposition5.17, without importing its matrix proof. It does not
answer the different general column-permutation question in that paper.

In the even row of S1, Hall puts 2 in H, so the unique entry one is
at coset H. In the odd row, D=H union{0}, so H is SUM-FREE:
a+b=c with a,b,c in H would give the two distinct nonzero ordered
representations c-a=b-0, a contradiction. Thus R(H)=0 and 2H!=H.

In this odd row, 3 divides q=|H|. Let omega be a nontrivial cube
root in H. Every zero-sum triple in H consists of exactly one
multiplicative coset of {1,omega,omega²}. To prove it, divide
a+b+c=0 by c and use
\(a/c+b/c=-1=\omega+\omega^2\), together with the pair-sum
uniqueness just proved. Conversely every such triple sums to zero.
All its entries are distinct because p!=3. Hence there are q/3
unordered such triples, 2q ordered triples, and R(z)=2 on -H.
In particular 2H differs from BOTH H and -H. These statements
concern actual H, not a proposed arbitrary union of cosets.
