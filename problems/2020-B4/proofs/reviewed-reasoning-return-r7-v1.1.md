## R7A. Exact joint integral-phase criterion at every modulus

For every divisor d>1 of v choose an algebraic integer

    alpha_d in Z[zeta_d],    alpha_d*bar(alpha_d)=q,

where zeta_v is fixed primitive and zeta_d=zeta_v^(v/d). For each residue
x modulo v set

    N_x = k + sum_(d|v,d>1) Tr_(Q(zeta_d)/Q)(alpha_d*zeta_d^(-x)),
    a_x = N_x/v.                                               (A1)

Every N_x is an ordinary integer: each summand is the trace of an
algebraic integer. Then the following statements are equivalent:

1. Every N_x is divisible by v.
2. A=sum_x a_x X^x is the indicator of an actual cyclic (v,k,1)
   difference set, with its order-d primitive character value alpha_d
   for every d>1.

In particular, once the full coherent phase tuple is jointly integral,
binary coefficients and nonnegativity follow; they are not additional
unproved conditions.

### Proof

For j!=0 modulo v write uniquely

    j=(v/d)*b,    d>1 divides v, b in (Z/dZ)^*.

Prescribe F(0)=k and F(j)=sigma_b(alpha_d), where
sigma_b(zeta_d)=zeta_d^b. Equation (A1) is precisely inverse discrete
Fourier transformation, since summing over b gives the displayed trace.
Indeed sum_x zeta_v^(jx) is v for j=0 and zero otherwise; applying this
orthogonality twice proves both inversion and uniqueness. Thus

    sum_x a_x=k,    A(zeta_v^j)=F(j).

All a_x are rational. Moreover F(-j)=bar(F(j)), and applying sigma_b
to alpha_d*bar(alpha_d)=q gives F(j)F(-j)=q for j!=0. The principal
value satisfies k^2=q+v. Orthogonality, or injectivity of all characters
on Q[G], now gives the EXACT rational group-ring identity

    A A^-=q*1+N_G.                                            (A2)

The coefficient at 0 in (A2) is sum_x a_x^2=q+1=k. If all N_x are
divisible by v, all a_x are integers, and hence

    sum_x a_x(a_x-1)=0.

Each term is nonnegative for EVERY integer a_x, including negative
integers. Therefore every term is zero and a_x is 0 or 1. Augmentation
gives k distinct support points; nonzero coefficients of (A2) give each
ordered difference once. This proves 1=>2. Conversely an actual D has
the stated character values and inverse Fourier coefficients, so its
indicator satisfies (A1) and 1. This proves the equivalence.

The statement concerns the COMPLETE order-indexed tuple. A choice of
one primitive character at composite v omits other character orders.
Even actual proper quotient images do not remove this distinction; see
R7E below.

### Known multiplier subgroup and one common normalization

Suppose every alpha_d is fixed by sigma_(t mod d) for every t in a
fixed T<=(Z/vZ)^*. This assertion means invariance under the image of
T in the order-d Galois group. The inverse coefficients then satisfy
a_(tx)=a_x. For an integral tuple the reconstructed D is T-stable.
Its sum S=sum_(x in D)x lies in the COMMON fixed subgroup G^T. The
unique sum-zero translation c=-k^(-1)S also lies in G^T. Thus D+c
remains T-stable. On phases this multiplies alpha_d by zeta_d^c;
its T-invariance follows from (t-1)c=0 modulo v, for every t in T.
This is a common-subgroup assertion, not a substitution of individual
fixed groups for their intersection.

## R7B. Prime modulus removes the remaining integral-lift condition

Assume p=v=q^2+q+1 is PRIME. Let K=Q(zeta_p), O_K=Z[zeta_p], and
lambda=1-zeta_p. Then an actual cyclic (p,k,1) difference set exists
if and only if there exists beta in O_K with

    beta*bar(beta)=q.                                         (B1)

More precisely, for each beta satisfying (B1) exactly one of beta,-beta
has residue k in O_K/(lambda)=F_p. Call it alpha. There is a UNIQUE
element A in Z[C_p] with A(zeta_p)=alpha and augmentation k, and that
A is the indicator of an actual difference set.

### Integral lift and binary recovery

The classical integral basis O_K=Z[zeta_p] is an attributed cyclotomic
fact. The residue map zeta_p=>1 identifies O_K/(lambda) with F_p,
because Phi_p(1)=p. Conjugation induces the identity on this residue
field. Reducing (B1) gives beta^2=q=k^2 modulo lambda. Here p>=7 is
odd and k is nonzero modulo p. The two roots are exactly k and -k,
so the sign choice is unique.

Write alpha=sum_(i=0)^(p-2) b_i zeta_p^i with b_i integral, set b_(p-1)=0,
and put s=sum_i b_i. Reduction modulo lambda gives s=k modulo p.
Add (k-s)/p to EVERY b_i. This leaves evaluation at zeta_p unchanged
and changes augmentation to k. It gives an integral A.

For a representative polynomial of degree at most p-1, vanishing at
zeta_p means being a rational multiple of Phi_p(X); if its coefficients
are integers, that multiple is an integer by its leading coefficient.
Consequently the kernel Z[C_p]=>O_K is exactly Z*N_G. This also proves
uniqueness of the lift with augmentation k. Since A A^--q*1 evaluates
to zero, it equals c*N_G for an integer c. Augmentation gives

    c*p=k^2-q=p,

so c=1. The coefficient-at-zero/augmentation argument of R7A now
forces A to be binary. Conversely an actual D supplies (B1). No
saturation, unit orbit, classical plane or residual-size hypothesis
appears in this proof.

### Exact fixed-field version and full numerical group

Let T<=F_p^* and F=K^T. There exists a T-stable actual difference set
if and only if there exists beta in O_F with (B1). The sign stays in F.
For each t in T the uniquely lifted indicators for sigma_t(alpha) and
alpha have the same augmentation; evaluation injectivity at fixed
augmentation therefore proves tD=D. The converse follows by evaluating
a T-stable indicator. This is an EXACT existence criterion in the
specified field, not a claim that such a beta always exists.

If T is nonidentity, G^T={0} because p is prime. Thus the sum of this
T-stable D is already zero. For ANY actual D in the common sum-zero
frame, evaluation and fixed-augmentation injectivity give

    M = {t in F_p^*: sigma_t(alpha)=alpha}
      = Gal(K/Q(alpha)).                                    (B2)

For the first equality a numerical multiplier first fixes D setwise:
if tD=D+c, summing gives k*c=0 and c=0. Formula (B2) concerns the
numerical multiplier group, not the full automorphism group of a plane.

### Auxiliary norm-one lemma, without an unproved unit assumption

For any odd prime p and u in Z[zeta_p] with u*bar(u)=1, the same residue
argument gives a unique sign w=+/-u with residue 1. Lift w integrally
with augmentation 1. The kernel argument yields U U^--1=c*N_G;
augmentation forces c=0. Thus sum coefficients squared is 1 and their
sum is 1, so U is a single positive basis element. Hence

    u=+/-zeta_p^c.                                           (B3)

If u lies in K^T for a nonidentity T, then (t-1)c=0 modulo p for a
nonidentity t, so c=0 and u=+/-1. This proves only the norm-one claim;
it does not make every totally positive unit a unit norm.

## R7C. Exact ideal-and-unit obligations in the prime fixed field

Keep prime p=v and a specified T<=F_p^*. Put F=K^T; complex conjugation
acts on F because the whole cyclotomic Galois group is abelian. Use
the ordinary ring of integers O_F. There is an element alpha in O_F
with alpha*bar(alpha)=q if and only if there is an integral ideal a of
O_F satisfying all THREE conditions:

    a*bar(a)=(q);                                           (C1)
    a=(gamma) is principal;                                 (C2)
    for epsilon=gamma*bar(gamma)/q, a unit u satisfies
          u*bar(u)=epsilon^(-1).                            (C3)

If (C1)--(C2) hold, epsilon is automatically a unit of O_F fixed by
conjugation, positive under every real embedding of the fixed real
field: the ideals of its numerator and denominator agree, and its
embedded values are |sigma(gamma)|^2/q>0. This positivity does NOT
establish (C3). Different choices of gamma change the written epsilon,
but existence in (C3) is independent of that choice.

Indeed alpha gives a=(alpha), gamma=alpha and u=1. Conversely set
alpha=u*gamma. It is integral and its conjugation norm is q. If a is
already fixed, any two such generators differ by a unit of norm one.
When T is nonidentity, (B3) says they differ by a sign only. This is
a statement about a fixed ideal, not a bound on the number of ideals
or on all possible difference sets.

For clarity, at an unramified rational prime ell dividing q, write
a=product_P P^(a_P). Equation (C1) requires

    a_P+a_(bar(P))=v_ell(q)

at primes P over ell. A self-conjugate P requires even v_ell(q).
This classical self-conjugacy obstruction remains a necessary ideal
condition. If T contains every prime-divisor multiplier ell|q, then
each such ell splits completely in F: p does not divide ell, its
cyclotomic Frobenius is sigma_ell, and restriction to F is trivial.
When T has odd order, conjugation is nontrivial and these split primes
occur in distinct conjugate pairs. Even then (C2) and (C3) do not
follow from splitting or from (C1). The next example proves a failure
of (C2) in a relevant nonsaturated fixed field.

## R7D. An explicit balanced nonprincipal ideal at q=5

Take q=5, p=31 and T=<5><=F_31^*. Its order is 3 because
5^3=125=1 modulo31 and 5!=1. Let F=Q(zeta_31)^T; then [F:Q]=10.
The known subgroup T has size 3<q. Nothing here assumes saturation.
The known existence of a classical order-five example is attributed
background; the calculation below constructs no preliminary example.

Let R be the subgroup of quadratic residues modulo31. It has order15
and contains T: every element of odd order in the cyclic group F_31^*
is a square. Write E=Q(zeta_31)^R. Then E is quadratic, E subset F,
and [F:E]=5. We identify E and the needed ideal directly.

Let chi be the quadratic character modulo31 and
tau=sum_(x!=0) chi(x)*zeta_31^x. For a nonzero a, substitution gives
sigma_a(tau)=chi(a)*tau. Also chi(-1)=-1, so bar(tau)=-tau. Replacing
x by t*y in the product tau*bar(tau) gives

    tau*bar(tau)
      = sum_(t!=0) chi(t) sum_(y!=0) zeta_31^((t-1)*y)
      =30-sum_(t!=0,1) chi(t)=31.

Thus tau^2=-31; tau is nonrational and fixed by R. Therefore

    E=Q(sqrt(-31)),  omega=(1+sqrt(-31))/2,
    O_E=Z[omega],   omega^2-omega+8=0.                      (D1)

Here is also the integral-basis check in (D1). If an element is integral,
write it as (a+b*sqrt(-31))/2 with a integral (its trace) and b rational.
Its norm gives a^2+31b^2 in4Z. For b=m/n in lowest terms this forces
n^2|31, hence n=1. Reduction modulo4 then makes a,b have the same
parity. Conversely omega satisfies the displayed monic polynomial.

The two maps O_E=>F_5 given by omega=>2 and omega=>4 have distinct
kernels

    P=(5,omega-2), Q=(5,omega-4)=bar(P).

Both ideals have norm5. They are comaximal, since their generators
give both2 and5 in P+Q. Modulo5 the polynomial in (D1) is
(X-2)(X-4); hence, equivalently by the two residue maps,

    (5)=P Q.                                                (D2)

Set gamma=4*omega-3=-1+2*sqrt(-31). Its norm is125. Its residue at P
is0, whereas its residue at Q is13=3 modulo5. Unique ideal
factorization and the principal-ideal norm formula therefore give

    (gamma)=P^3.                                            (D3)

However P is NOT principal. A generator would be an algebraic integer
of norm5, so its integers a,b as in (D1) would satisfy

    a^2+31b^2=20.

If b!=0 the left side is at least31; if b=0 no integer square is20.
Thus the ideal class of P has exact order3.

Extend it to a=P*O_F. From (D2),

    a*bar(a)=(5)*O_F,                                      (D4)

so a is an integral balanced ideal of the exact type (C1). Nevertheless
a is NONPRINCIPAL. The relative ideal norm for the degree-five
extension F/E satisfies

    N_(F/E)(P*O_F)=P^5,
    N_(F/E)((eta))=(N_(F/E)(eta)).                          (D5)

These are standard ideal-norm identities, with their hypotheses and
published proofs checked in Milne Proposition4.1(a),(c). One may see
the first directly by factoring P*O_F into primes: its norm exponent
is sum e_i*f_i=[F:E]=5. If a were principal, (D5) would make P^5
principal. But P^3 is principal and gcd(3,5)=1, which would make P
principal, contradicting the preceding paragraph.

This is a hand-proved obstruction to the proposed bridge
"a balanced ideal in the multiplier fixed field has a norm-q generator."
It does not rule out other balanced PRINCIPAL ideals in F, and it does
not rule out actual q=5 difference sets, which are known to exist.
No class-group computation or complete class-number assertion is used.

## R7E. Composite-modulus phases can fail even over actual quotients

Take q=16, v=273=3*7*13, k=17 and zeta=zeta_273. The integral element

    alpha_273=4

has conjugation norm16 and is fixed by every numerical Galois action.
Nevertheless there is NO subset B of 17 distinct residues in C_273
such that

    sum_(b in B) zeta^b=4.                                 (E1)

No difference-set or multiplier assumption is needed for this
impossibility.

### Trace obstruction

For x in C_273, its cyclotomic trace is the Ramanujan sum

    c(x)=Tr_(Q(zeta)/Q)(zeta^x)
        = product_(ell=3,7,13)
             [(ell-1) if ell divides x, and -1 otherwise].  (E2)

For completeness, apply the Chinese remainder theorem to the units
in the trace sum sum_(a in(Z/273)^*) zeta^(a*x). It factors into the
three sums over nonzero residues modulo ell. Those sums are ell-1
when ell|x and -1 otherwise, which proves (E2).

At x=0 this is phi(273)=2*6*12=144. If x!=0 and c(x)>0, precisely
two of the prime factors of273 do not divide x, so

    c(x)<=144/(2*6)=12.

If c(x) is negative the same upper bound holds. Thus for any 17-set B,

    Tr(sum_(b in B) zeta^b)<=144+16*12=336,

where the bound also covers 0 notin B, when 17*12 is even smaller.
But Tr(4)=4*144=576. This contradicts (E1).

Even a nonnegative integral lift of the primitive component is not
enough without the other component norms. Let H=21Z/273Z, the subgroup
of order13, and form the multiset group-ring element

    A=4*X^0+sum_(h in H) X^h.

It has augmentation17, is fixed by all units, has weighted group sum0
(21*(0+...+12)=1638=6*273), and A(zeta)=4. Its coefficient at0 is5,
so it is not a subset. At a character of order3 it has value17 and
fails the required norm16. This example is not an input to Cohen's
actual-subset theorem or the actual full-multiplier size bound.

### Stronger failure with every proper quotient already actual

Use attributed known Singer existence at q=16 to choose an ACTUAL
cyclic planar D0, put it in the common sum-zero frame, and let
T=<2><=(Z/273)^*. The First Multiplier Theorem makes D0 T-stable.
The order of T is12: 2^12=1 modulo273, while 2^6!=1 and 2^4!=1,
which exclude every proper divisor of12.

For EVERY proper divisor d>1 of273, set

    alpha_d=sum_(x in D0) zeta_d^x,

keep principal value17, and replace ONLY the order273 component by4.
Each alpha_d is an algebraic integer of conjugation norm16, and every
component is fixed by the image of T. Within each order its other
values are the required Galois conjugates. ALL proper quotient images
are jointly the actual images of the ONE D0; this is stronger than
merely passing a collection of quotient norm equations.

Indeed the rational inverse A from R7A has, at every character of
every proper quotient C_d, the same value as the image of D0. Fourier
injectivity on that quotient identifies its entire coefficient vector
with that actual quotient image. None of those quotient characters
has order273. Despite this, the full tuple has NO actual subset lift:
any such lift would satisfy the impossible (E1). By R7A at least one
of its full inverse coefficients fails to be an integer.

Consequently the proposed realization rule

    actual coherent proper quotients + an integral primitive
    norm-q phase + known multiplier invariance => actual D

is false, even at a known prime-power order and even in the correct
common frame. This is not a nonexistence assertion for q=16. It exposes
the missing global integral gluing condition while retaining actual
proper quotient data.
