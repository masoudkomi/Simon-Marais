## 1. R9A: at least four odd interior runs for every actual selection

The only new external theorem imported here is Atkinson--Sharma's
Theorem2, *A partial characterization of poised Hermite-Birkhoff
interpolation problems*, SIAM J. Numer. Anal.6(1969),230--235,
[author-hosted text](https://homepage.math.uiowa.edu/~atkinson/papers/HB_interp.pdf).
Its sufficient criterion uses an incidence matrix with n ones for
polynomials of degree at most n-1: the Pólya cumulative inequalities,
together with conservativeness, imply that the only homogeneous
solution is zero at every strictly ordered real node set. A row run
starting at derivative order0 is conservative; a positive-order odd
run is forbidden only if its node lies strictly inside the span of
nodes occurring at earlier orders. The source's proposed CONVERSE is
not used. The published proof is an attributed import.

Here is the complete adaptation. It holds already for any nonzero REAL
polynomial F of degree20 with four distinct real nodes W, each a root
of F, two specified distinct nodes u,v roots of F', and an actual
witness w_k in W for every derivative k=2,...,19. It does not require
that all roots of F be among W.

For an arbitrary such selection w_2,...,w_19, form an incidence matrix E:

* All four rows have an entry at order0.
* The rows u,v both have an entry at order1.
* For each k=2,...,19, precisely the chosen row w_k has an entry at k.
* There are no other entries. Additional actual zeros are deliberately
  not entered in this selected matrix.

Write W={x1<x2<x3<x4} in ORDINARY REAL order. A positive interior run
is a maximal consecutive string of entries in row x2 or x3 whose
first order is positive. Count only the runs of odd length, and let K
be their number, across both rows. Entries at order0 are part of their
initial run and do not count as a positive run.

**R9A. Every actual selection has K>=4.**

Proof. The matrix has24 ones: four at0, two at1, and eighteen at
orders2,...,19. Its original cumulative column sums are

    M0=4; M_p=p+5 for1<=p<=19.                             (1)

Suppose K<=3. In each odd positive interior run delete its LAST entry.
A singleton vanishes and a longer odd run becomes even. Deleting an
endpoint cannot join two runs or create a new separated run. No entry
at order0 is removed. The remaining incidence matrix E' has n=24-K
ones, and n>=21. Give it columns0,...,n-1 by padding columns above19
with zeros. For p<=19 its cumulative sum is at least M_p-K, so it
is >=p+1; at p0 it remains4. For20<=p<=n-1 its cumulative sum is n,
again >=p+1. These are ALL the Pólya inequalities, including the
previously empty highest columns.

Every one of the four nodes is still entered at order0. Consequently
the earlier-order span in the definition of conservativeness is always
[x1,x4] for a positive run. Boundary rows are conservative, initial
order0 runs are conservative, and every remaining positive interior
run has even length. Thus E' is conservative. Atkinson--Sharma implies
that its homogeneous interpolation problem on degree<=n-1 has only
the zero solution. But F is a nonzero such solution: its actual zeros
contain all remaining entries and deg(F)=20<=n-1. Contradiction.
This proves R9A.

In our branch take u=0,v=s. Their ACTUAL double multiplicities justify
the two order1 entries. Rationality supplies real nodes. No Rolle or
real-rootedness assumption is placed on g. The result applies to every
permitted choice among multiple common values, not only to one
canonical scenario or to derivatives with unique witnesses.
