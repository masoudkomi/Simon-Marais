## Descent check and its exact limitation

From the displayed normalized specialization, coefficientwise squaring
sends P_alpha to P_(alpha^2): unique factorization identifies the factor,
and the monic specialization removes a possible scalar. Its coefficient
of X^q in the specialization is alpha, so its coefficient field must
contain alpha. The minimal coefficient field is therefore exactly the
field generated by alpha, not automatically the entire F_q.

One Frobenius orbit of labels consequently gives one irreducible F2
orbit product. The prior conditional orbit argument is valid on this
premise and explains why the n=13 absolute splitting alone is not an F2
factorization. For k≥3 there is more than one orbit, since 2^k−2>k.
This is a particular family consequence of the attributed decomposition,
not an unrestricted reducibility classification. No statement about APN
functions supplies a missing F2 polynomial factorization.

## A precise later source inference that is not imported

The 2016 preprint's Lemma 1 proof, PDF pages 8–9, uses the parity of
the number of nonconstant equal-degree terms X^mY^m in a symmetric
product. Its algebraic expression is

    t_A t_B+t_A+t_B modulo 2.

The proof asserts that this is one only if both t_A,t_B are one.
In fact it is also one for (t_A,t_B)=(1,0) and (0,1). There is an
elementary counterexample to that inference within symmetric
constant-one polynomials:

    A=1+X+Y, B=1+XY,
    AB=1+X+Y+XY+X^2Y+XY^2.

A has no nonconstant equal-degree term, B has one, and their product
has one. Moreover A(1,1)=1 while B(1,1)=0. Thus the parity calculation
does not force every symmetric factor through (1,1).

This disproves that proof inference, not the Kasami decomposition and
not the truth of its later intersection theorem. Extra special
hypotheses might repair the later theorem, but no repair is assumed.
The new T1–T4 argument imports neither Lemma 1 nor the claim that
every Kasami component passes through (1,1).
