## Hand obstruction in the standard dehomogenization

Work in characteristic two, odd n>=5. Put d=n-3 and

    F(x,y)=phi_n(x,y,1),
    H(x,y)=phi_n(x,y,0).

Expand the homogeneous polynomial phi_n by powers of z:

    phi_n(x,y,z)=H(x,y)+z J(x,y)+terms divisible by z²,

where H has degree d and J is zero or homogeneous of degree d-1.
Both numerator and denominator of the defining quotient are polynomial
identities; coefficient comparison involves no division at a singular point.
The denominator expands as

    (x+y)(x+z)(y+z)
       =xy(x+y)+z(x+y)²+z²(x+y).

The coefficient of z in the numerator is (x+y)^(n-1), since n is odd
and n>1. Therefore

    xy(x+y) J+(x+y)² H=(x+y)^(n-1).

Cancellation of the nonzero polynomial x+y in the polynomial integral
domain gives

    xy J+(x+y)H=(x+y)^(n-2).                         (1)

The form H is nonzero and H(1,1)=1. For the latter value, substitute
x=1+u,y=1 into its exact defining identity. The numerator is
(1+u)^n+1+u^n, with lowest term u of coefficient1. Its denominator
is (1+u)u, also with lowest term u of coefficient1. Consequently the
polynomial quotient has constant term1. This justifies evaluation of H,
without substituting into an undefined rational expression.

If J=0, (1) would imply H=(x+y)^(n-3). At (1,1) the latter is0,
since n-3>=2, contradicting H(1,1)=1. Thus J is NONZERO.

There is also a nonzero constant form in F: phi_n(0,0,1)=1.
Indeed, in formal x,y near zero the numerator's first homogeneous term
is x+y, while the denominator's is x+y; comparing their degree-one
parts in the polynomial identity makes the quotient's constant1.

Hence the standard F has a nonzero degree-d form H, a nonzero degree
d-1 form J, and constant1. There are at least three nonzero homogeneous
forms. Its first gap is1 and its last gap is d>=2. Every nonnegative
integer lies in the additive semigroup generated by1. The final-gap
nonmembership hypothesis of the preprint's Theorem8 therefore FAILS in
this standard chart for every odd n>=5, regardless of the other hypotheses.
The later-gap alternative in Theorem12 likewise yields no gap beyond1
