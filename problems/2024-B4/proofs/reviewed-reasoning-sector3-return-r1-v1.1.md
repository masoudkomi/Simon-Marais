## 1. The local fact used twice

Let T(x,y) be a nonzero polynomial with local order d>=1 at the origin.
Suppose its lowest homogeneous term is c L^d, c!=0, where L is one
nonzero linear form, and its homogeneous term of degree d+1 is not
divisible by L. Then a factorization T=UV cannot have U(0)=V(0)=0.

Indeed write the lowest terms of U,V as U_a,V_b with a,b>=1. Since
U_a V_b=c L^d, unique factorization gives a+b=d and
U_a=c_1 L^a,V_b=c_2 L^b. The term of degree d+1 of their product is

    U_a V_(b+1)+U_(a+1) V_b,

where a missing term is zero. Both summands are divisible by L,
contradiction. For d1 the two positive orders are already impossible.
In particular, if a factor vanishes at the point then its cofactor is
a unit in the local ring and its local order equals d. This is the
single-tangent, consecutive-coprime-terms mechanism of the attributed
PropositionA; both hypotheses are retained, and the proof is given.

## 2. Complete reconstruction of the published sector theorem

Fix m=2ell+1>3 with ell>=3 odd. Put

    f(x,y)=f_m(x,y,1), w(x,y)=(x+1)(y+1)(x+y),
    g(x,y)=phi_m(x,y,1), so f=w g.

### 2.1 Every possible singular point of g is classified

If g and both its first partials vanish at P=(alpha,beta), the product
rule shows that f and both its first partials vanish there as well.
Set lambda=alpha+beta+1. Since m is odd,

    f_x(P)=alpha^(2ell)+lambda^(2ell),
    f_y(P)=beta^(2ell)+lambda^(2ell).

Injectivity of squaring in K makes alpha^ell=beta^ell=lambda^ell=u.
The equation f(P)=0 becomes

    0=(alpha+beta+lambda) u^2+1=u^2+1.

Thus u=1 and alpha,beta,lambda are NONZERO ell-th roots of unity.
Conversely those three root conditions give f=f_x=f_y=0. We do not
initially assume any of the three coordinates nonzero; this proves it.

Use local increments x,y at P and write f(P+(x,y))=F_2+F_3+... .
The binomial coefficients binom(m,2) and binom(m,3) are both1 in F2
because the two lowest bits of m are11. Using the root conditions gives

    F_2=(alpha^(-1)+lambda^(-1)) x^2
          +(beta^(-1)+lambda^(-1)) y^2,

    F_3=(alpha^(-2)+lambda^(-2)) x^3
          +(beta^(-2)+lambda^(-2)) y^3
          +lambda^(-2)(x^2 y+x y^2).                   (1)

All possible points fall into exactly the following three cases.

**TypeI:** alpha=beta=lambda=1. Here F_2=0, F_3=xy(x+y), while the
translated denominator is EXACTLY xy(x+y). Comparing the lowest
terms of f=w g gives g(P)=1. This point is not on the quotient curve.

**TypeII:** alpha=1,beta=b!=1; or beta=1,alpha=b!=1; or
alpha=beta=b!=1,lambda=1. In the first case the denominator has lowest
term (b+1)^2 x and F_2=(1+b^(-1))x^2, so g has a NONZERO linear
term proportional to x. The second case is the exchanged computation.
In the third case the denominator has lowest term (b+1)^2(x+y),
and F_2=(1+b^(-1))(x+y)^2, so the quotient has a nonzero linear
term proportional to x+y. All these points on g are smooth.

**TypeIII:** alpha,beta are neither1 and alpha!=beta. Then lambda
is neither1, and

    w_0=(alpha+1)(beta+1)(alpha+beta)!=0.

Consequently g has order2. The following calculation rules out the
intersection of two factors at every such point.

### 2.2 The cubic misses the unique double tangent

Choose A,B in K with

    A^2=alpha^(-1)+lambda^(-1)=(beta+1)/(alpha lambda),
    B^2=beta^(-1)+lambda^(-1)=(alpha+1)/(beta lambda).

K is algebraically closed, so such square roots exist. Both A and B
are nonzero, and

    (A+B)^2=(alpha+beta)/(alpha beta)!=0.

Thus F_2=(Ax+By)^2 has exactly one tangent L=Ax+By. In (1) its
cubic term is

    F_3=A^4 x^3+B^4 y^3+lambda^(-2)(x^2 y+x y^2).

At the nonzero vector (B,A) on L=0,

    F_3(B,A)=AB(A+B)[A^2 B^2+lambda^(-2)],
    A^2 B^2+lambda^(-2)=1/(alpha beta lambda)!=0.       (2)

The last identity follows by expanding the product: its numerator
after multiplication by alpha beta lambda is alpha+beta+lambda=1.
Every factor on the right of (2) is nonzero. Therefore L does NOT
divide F_3.

Write the quotient terms as G_2,G_3 and the translated denominator as
w_0+w_1+... . Multiplication yields

    G_2=F_2/w_0,
    w_0 G_3=F_3+w_1 G_2.                              (3)

The second summand in (3) is divisible by L, whereas F_3 is not.
So G_2 is a nonzero scalar times L^2 and L does not divide G_3.
The local fact of Section1 prohibits two vanishing factors of g.
No unibranch assertion beyond this explicit polynomial fact is needed.

### 2.3 Projective completion, including infinity and repetitions

The homogeneous phi_m is symmetric in X,Y,Z. At ANY projective point
one of the three coordinates is nonzero. Permute that coordinate into
the Z position and scale it to1. The SAME affine analysis then applies.
Consequently there is no projective point at which two factors of
phi_m can both vanish: it would be singular, TypeI is absent, TypeII
is smooth and TypeIII is forbidden by (3). This handles points at
infinity without assuming that an affine computation already covered
them, and without transporting a local statement through a noninvertible
map. Coordinate permutations and the local affine chart are invertible.

Suppose phi_m=U V over K with both factors positive degree. Factors
of a homogeneous polynomial can be taken homogeneous: comparing the
lowest and highest nonzero total degrees of each factor forces each
factor to have only one degree. If U,V have no common component,
Bézout's theorem gives a projective intersection since degU degV>0.
If they share a component, that projective curve itself has points
over K, also giving an intersection. At an intersection both local
factors vanish, contradicting the preceding paragraph. The shared-
component case is explicitly included; squarefreeness is not being
assumed in order to exclude repeated factors.

Hence the WHOLE phi_m is irreducible over K. This proves S3A as an
attributed reconstruction, and ordinary F2 irreducibility follows.
No Gold, Kasami or exceptional-APN classification was used.

## 3. Exact diagonal germ for an arbitrary odd target

Now n>=3 is ANY odd integer, with n=Qh+1 as in S3B. Differentiating
the already established identity f_n=R phi_n in Y and then putting
Y=Z=1 gives the polynomial identity

    (X+1)^2 phi_n(X,1,1)=X^(n-1)+1.                   (4)

In particular at a!=1,

    phi_n(a,1,1)=0  iff  a^(n-1)=1  iff  a^h=1.       (5)

The last equivalence uses injectivity of the Q-th power. Such an a
is nonzero. If h=1, (5) would force a=1; thus under our hypotheses h>=3.
This also checks that the terms through Q+1 below are within the degree
of the polynomial, including the small n=3/unit case, which has no such
point at all.

Fix a satisfying (5), put c=a+1!=0, and use EXACT local coordinates

    X=a+x, Y=1+y, Z=1;          y=0 is the diagonal Y=Z.

The translated denominator is

    w=(c+x)y(c+x+y)
      =c^2 y+c y^2+x^2 y+x y^2
      =w_1+w_2+w_3.                                    (6)

There is no constant term. In the numerator the constant and linear
terms vanish, and every term of degree2,...,Q-1 vanishes. One can see
this from

    (b+z)^n=(b+z)(b^Q+z^Q)^h,

since h is odd. Let E=a^(-Q)!=0 and b=1+a^(1-Q). The first two
possible terms of the numerator are EXACTLY

    F_Q=b y^Q,
    F_(Q+1)=y[E x^Q+E x y^(Q-1)+(1+E)y^Q].            (7)

These formulas use a^h=1, so a^(n-Q)=a^(1-Q) and
a^(n-Q-1)=a^(-Q). There is no pure x term in (7), because the
two occurrences with base a cancel. No general singularity table is
being assumed for this arbitrary target.

Write the local quotient as T=phi_n(a+x,1+y,1). If b!=0, its
lowest degree is Q-1 and comparison of w_1 T_(Q-1)=F_Q gives

    T_(Q-1)=(b/c^2)y^(Q-1).

At the very next degree, w_1 T_Q+w_2 T_(Q-1)=F_(Q+1), whence

    T_Q=(E/c^2)x^Q+(E/c^2)xy^(Q-1)
            +[(1+E)/c^2+b/c^3]y^Q.                    (8)

In particular its x^Q coefficient E/c^2 is NONZERO. The correction
from w_2 T_(Q-1) affects only y^Q; it cannot cancel x^Q. Thus the
unique initial tangent y does not divide the next term. Section1
applies, proving that at most one factor can vanish here, and that its
cofactor is a unit and its multiplicity equals Q-1.

If b=0, the quotient has order Q instead, and its lowest term is

    T_Q=(E/c^2)x^Q+(E/c^2)xy^(Q-1)+(1+E)/c^2 y^Q.      (9)

Again E/c^2!=0, so y does not divide this initial term. Any factor
that is smooth here with tangent y would force such a divisibility,
which is impossible. Finally

    b=0 iff a^(Q-1)=1 iff a^Q=a iff a is in F_Q.

These two cases prove every assertion of S3B, with the actual tangent
frame and the quotient correction retained.
