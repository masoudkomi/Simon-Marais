## 2. The local factor argument retains the needed next term

Suppose the initial homogeneous part of a nonzero local polynomial T
is c L^d and the next part is not divisible by L. In a hypothetical
factorization with both factors vanishing, their orders a,b are positive
and sum to d. Their initial parts multiply to c L^d in K[x,y], so they
are scalar powers L^a and L^b. Both summands contributing to degree
d+1 are then multiples of L. This contradicts the stated next part.
For d=1 the order equation already contradicts a,b>=1.

This proves the precise conclusion used later: if U divides T and
U vanishes, the cofactor V has nonzero value at the point. Thus V is
a unit of the local ring and ord(T)=ord(U). The argument works even
when U is reducible; it asserts no unwarranted analytic unibranchness.
It also avoids applying intersection multiplicity to two factors with
a shared component. The thesis's Proposition A has the same two
operative hypotheses. The candidate proves the needed polynomial fact
directly, rather than quoting just its conclusion.

The next-term condition is essential. In characteristic two,
T=y^2+x^4=(y+x^2)^2 has just one initial tangent, yet both displayed
factors vanish. Its degree-three term is zero, which is divisible by y,
so it does not meet the candidate's hypothesis. This attempted

## 3. Whole absolute irreducibility in the published sector

Write m=2ell+1 with ell odd, ell>=3. In the Z=1 chart let f=w g,
with w=(x+1)(y+1)(x+y). A singular point of g is a singular point
of f by the product rule, including when w vanishes; no division by w
is made at this step. Put lambda=alpha+beta+1 at such a point.
The two first derivatives give

    alpha^ell=beta^ell=lambda^ell=u.

Substitution into f=0 gives u^2+1=0 because alpha+beta+lambda=1.
Injectivity of squaring gives u=1. This derives nonzero alpha,beta,
lambda rather than assuming it. The three root conditions conversely
annul f and its first derivatives.

The bottom two bits of m are 11. Therefore the quadratic and cubic
binomial coefficients are both odd. Reduction using the root conditions
gives

    F2=(alpha^-1+lambda^-1)x^2+(beta^-1+lambda^-1)y^2,
    F3=(alpha^-2+lambda^-2)x^3+(beta^-2+lambda^-2)y^3
          +lambda^-2(x^2 y+x y^2).

There are exactly three denominator strata among these points.

* If alpha=beta=lambda=1, the entire translated denominator is
  xy(x+y). The leading numerator is that same cubic. Their identity
  f=w g forces g(P)=1, so this is not a point of the quotient curve.
* If alpha=1, beta=b!=1, the first denominator term is
  (b+1)^2 x and F2=(1+b^-1)x^2. The quotient therefore has nonzero
  linear term (1+b^-1)/(b+1)^2 times x. Exchanging alpha,beta gives
  the second pattern. If alpha=beta=b!=1, the denominator's first
  term is (b+1)^2(x+y) and F2=(1+b^-1)(x+y)^2. Its quotient is
  likewise smooth. These exhaust w(P)=0 outside the first case.
* Otherwise alpha,beta are distinct and neither is 1. Then lambda
  is also not 1 and w0=(alpha+1)(beta+1)(alpha+beta) is nonzero.
  The quotient has order two.

For the last stratum choose the unique square roots A,B of the two
quadratic coefficients in K. Their squares are
(beta+1)/(alpha lambda), (alpha+1)/(beta lambda), respectively;
both are nonzero. Also (A+B)^2=(alpha+beta)/(alpha beta) is nonzero.
Thus F2=L^2 with L=Ax+By and (B,A) is a nonzero point of L=0.
Directly, the cubic at that vector is

    F3(B,A)=AB(A+B)[A^2 B^2+lambda^-2]
           =AB(A+B)/(alpha beta lambda) != 0.

I checked the denominator in the last equality: expansion of the bracket
is 1/(alpha beta)+1/(alpha lambda)+1/(beta lambda), whose common
numerator is alpha+beta+lambda=1. Consequently L cannot divide F3.

The passage to the quotient is not F3/w0 alone. Multiplication gives
w0 G3=F3+w1 G2, with G2=F2/w0. The correction is divisible by L^2
and hence cannot remove the failure of L-divisibility. The local factor
argument applies to G2,G3 and excludes two vanishing factors there.

This classification did not presume that every singular point lies

## 4. Arbitrary odd targets: both diagonal alternatives

For any odd n>=3, differentiate the established identity f_n=R phi_n
in Y and set Y=Z=1. R vanishes there and R_Y=(X+1)^2, giving

    (X+1)^2 phi_n(X,1,1)=X^(n-1)+1.

Consequently any diagonal root a!=1 satisfies a^(n-1)=1. Write
n=Qh+1, Q=2^v2(n-1), h odd. Frobenius injectivity makes a^h=1.
Thus a is nonzero, and h=1 would force a=1; the asserted local setting
has h>=3. This simultaneously makes the Gold-type h=1 and the unit
n=3 cases vacuous. The terms through degree Q+1 are legitimate.

Take X=a+x, Y=1+y, Z=1, c=a+1. Here y=0 is the R-diagonal Y=Z,
not the coordinate boundary Y=0 in IC1. The denominator is exactly

    c^2 y+c y^2+x^2 y+x y^2.

In the numerator the constant/linear terms cancel, and
(a+u)^n=(a+u)(a^Q+u^Q)^h has no intermediate powers 2,...,Q-1.
The coefficients of u^Q and u^(Q+1) are a^(1-Q) and a^-Q.
Combining the two occurrences at base a cancels their pure x terms.
With E=a^-Q and b=1+a^(1-Q) the first two possible terms are

    FQ=b y^Q,
    F(Q+1)=E x^Q y+E x y^Q+(1+E)y^(Q+1).

These identities also apply when Q=2; the interval of missing degrees
is then empty. Since E!=0, the second term is a nonzero polynomial.

If b!=0, division by the degree-one denominator term gives order Q-1
and initial part (b/c^2)y^(Q-1). At the next degree the degree-two
denominator term must be retained. Its correction is (b/c^3)y^Q,
so the degree-Q quotient part is

    (E/c^2)x^Q+(E/c^2)x y^(Q-1)
       +[(1+E)/c^2+b/c^3]y^Q.

Its x^Q coefficient is nonzero. Thus the next term is not divisible
by the unique tangent y. The local factor lemma supplies the actual
unit-cofactor assertion and exact order Q-1 for any vanishing factor.

If b=0, the numerator instead has order Q+1. The quotient has order
Q with initial part

    (E/c^2)x^Q+(E/c^2)x y^(Q-1)+(1+E)y^Q/c^2.

This is not divisible by y either, so no smooth vanishing factor with
tangent y can divide it, regardless of the cofactor's order. There is
no claim that this branch has a unique component or a unit cofactor.
Finally b=0 iff a^(Q-1)=1 iff a^Q=a, precisely membership in F_Q.
This proves both stated alternatives without swapping their conclusions.

As a concrete boundary attack, take a^2+a+1=0 and n=13 (Q=4,h=3).
