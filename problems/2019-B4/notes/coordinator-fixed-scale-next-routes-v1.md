## 1. The mark-count issue may matter to the critical regime

The canonical c_n counts chronological pairs, equivalently physical
words with an admissible marked seed. The original b_(n+1) counts
unmarked words. They have the same root rate, but this does NOT make
their polynomial prefactors equal. In particular, a linear factor in
c_n could reflect many admissible seeds in a typical output rather
than a numerical failure of the renewal argument.

The accepted R7 alternatives include c_n R^n tending to infinity when
delta=0, linearly if its second moment is finite and sublinearly if
that moment is infinite. The accepted viable-mark bound is an UPPER
bound of order n, while words with a single viable seed also exist.
Neither side proves the typical aggregate number of viable marks.
Thus there is no present justification to favor delta>0 merely because
simple-pole asymptotics are familiar. The DIFFERENT critical-code
example already shows why generic nonoverlap alone cannot decide it.

A potentially useful new task is an exact or sufficiently uniform
aggregate analysis of admissible seed positions in the ORIGINAL
output language. It must retain arbitrary deletion choices. The old
explicit greedy counterexample `10001111001` remains in
`coordinator-unmarked-greedy-failure-v1.md`: a fixed preferred end
can fail although another removal order succeeds. No new greedy rule,
contiguous-interval property for the viable marks, or typical density
is established here. Reading a few valid seed examples cannot prove one.

## 2. A bidegree pressure route, with its missing inference exposed

For actual contexts of tape lengths a,b let M_(a,b) be their count.
For real theta one can investigate the positive partition sums

    Z_n(theta)=sum_(a+b=n) M_(a,b) exp(theta(a-b)).

Factoriality of canonical histories preserves the additive tape-length
weight and suggests weighted submultiplicativity. Tape swap gives
exact evenness in theta. The elementary bounds exp(-|theta|n)c_n
<=Z_n(theta)<=exp(|theta|n)c_n constrain a possible limiting pressure.
The usual finite log-sum-exp argument suggests convexity if the
all-n limit is first proved. These deductions have not been submitted
as a separately reviewed theorem and are not being used as one.

The hoped-for inference from symmetry to differentiability, strict
curvature, mixing or a positive renewal defect is MISSING. Even convex
even limiting functions can have a cusp at zero. Balanced tape pairs
already have the full rate by DG3, but that alone does not give a
positive polynomial fraction, Gaussian bidegree spread or the absence
of coexisting biased sectors.

The positive fixed-bidegree concatenation is real and injective because
the cuts are known. If blocks of swapped bidegrees are concatenated in
different orders, however, those cuts change. Their outputs may collide.
A binomial count of all orders therefore needs a genuinely recoverable
code before it can imply entropy gain. A simple first-bit tag can
distinguish TWO chosen block types, using the actual phase-zero
generators (0,1) and(1,0), but encoding every possible tape length costs
information. No bounded-cost all-bidegree code or uniform joining
theorem follows from this observation.

The accepted NR1 alternating-block budget supplies finite resets at
each fixed separation cap and constrains any full obstruction containing
a long alternating block. It does not supply a fixed universal reset
for the unrestricted language. Its prefix budget can grow with the
word being joined. Replacing that dependence by a constant would be
exactly a new, presently missing inference.

## 4. Other leads that have not become proofs

The accepted staircase has minimal upper-witness separation growing
with its index and length quadratic in that index. It is tempting to
conjecture a comparable universal lower length for every minimal
obstruction requiring large separation. A single family does not
prove that extremal assertion. The present argument has no universal
area/queue-growth estimate, so it cannot replace the accepted general
agreement through length2D+1 by quadratic agreement.

Existing length24 records were inspected as intuition, with no rerun
or larger enumeration. A broad text search printed too many matches
and was truncated; no claim of having reread that full output is made.
A subsequent exact text search for a displayed larger word beginning
RRR found none in those two existing records. This is finite evidence
only and no general prefix theorem is inferred from it. The next proof
would need to control the ACTUAL minimal upper witness, not a convenient
large-separation witness for the same pair.
