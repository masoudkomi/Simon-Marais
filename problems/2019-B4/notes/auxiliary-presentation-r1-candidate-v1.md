## 1. Exact object and accepted premise

Use the free monoid X* on X={A,B,C,D} and its homomorphism into the ordinary direct product of binary free monoids, with componentwise concatenation:

    A=(01,epsilon), B=(0,1), C=(1,0), D=(epsilon,01).

Let M0 be the IMAGE monoid, including the empty pair. A generator consumes two consecutive symbols of the alternating source, so its outputs are a chronological two-tape context. Only these EVEN phase-zero histories are used. We do not treat arbitrary odd histories as homomorphisms under ordinary pair concatenation.

Accepted secondpass-rigidity-obstructions.md Section1 proves that for p,q>=1 the pair (01(10)^q01,01(10)^p01) has EXACTLY TWO X histories:

    K=A C A^(q-1) B² D^(p-1) C D,
    H=D B D^(p-1) C² A^(q-1) B A.

They both have length p+q+4; K starts A and ends D, H starts D and ends A. Every proper contiguous X-factor of either has a unique image prehistory, since any nontrivial replacement would yield a third full history while preserving at least one distinct endpoint. **AF1 (classical finite-generator transport).** If a monoid M is generated by a finite set X and has SOME finite monoid presentation, its equality relation on X* is generated by finitely many sound X equations.

**AF2 (application).** M0 has NO finite monoid presentation on ANY finite alphabet. Consequently finitely many auxiliary generators presenting the SAME monoid cannot evade the accepted obstruction. In particular it has no finite complete rewriting system presenting that monoid and is not isomorphic to a trace monoid on a finite alphabet.

## 2. The standard transport lemma, with every substitution explicit

Suppose M is generated by finite X and is isomorphic to a finite monoid presentation <Y|R>, with finite alphabet Y and finite relation set R subset Y* x Y*. Fix this isomorphism. For every y in Y choose a word p_y in X* representing its image in M. For every x in X choose a word q_x in Y* representing x under the inverse isomorphism. These choices exist by generation/surjectivity. Empty words and identity-representing auxiliary generators are allowed. No algorithm for finding the choices or solving a word problem is asserted.

Extend the assignments to free-monoid homomorphisms p:Y*->X* and q:X*->Y*. Consider the finite relation set on X*

    S={ (p(u),p(v)) : (u,v) in R }
          union { (x,p(q_x)) : x in X }.

Every equation is SOUND in M. The first type is an image of an R equation under the chosen interpretation; the second represents x in M by construction. Thus the congruence generated by S is contained in actual equality.

Conversely suppose w,w' in X* represent the same M element. Their q-images are equal in the presented monoid <Y|R>. By the DEFINITION of a presented-monoid congruence, there is a FINITE derivation between q(w) and q(w') replacing either side of an R equation inside arbitrary left/right Y-word contexts. Applying the homomorphism p to every step gives a derivation by S equations inside the p-images of those contexts. A step whose p-images coincide can be omitted. This works for empty words and inserted/deleted factors as well.

The second type of equation, applied letter by letter, gives w congruent to p(q(w)); likewise w' congruent to p(q(w')). Compose these derivations with the transported one to get w congruent to w' modulo S. We have proved the reverse inclusion, hence S presents actual equality on X*. This proves AF1, with no assumption that p and q are literal inverse maps of FREE words.

This is the classical monoid Tietze change-of-generators mechanism. We supply the complete elementary proof rather than relying on an unchecked theorem about groups or on an unread external equivalence proof.

## 3. Applying the accepted rigid family, including identity equations

Assume M0 had a finite presentation on any finite Y. AF1 gives a finite set S of sound equations on X. If S is empty it clearly cannot identify distinct H and K. Otherwise let M be the largest X-word length of any side of an equation. Choose p,q>=1 with p+q+4>M.

Image total tape length is twice X-word length, so EVERY sound equation on X is length preserving. In particular an empty side can be sound only against another empty word. Such an equation has no effect. A nontrivial first change in a derivation starting from H would have to replace a matched factor of length<=M, which is a PROPER factor of H. Its image prehistory is unique by the accepted rigid-family property. Thus its replacement word must be the IDENTICAL factor, contrary to a change. Trivial steps can be omitted, so H is isolated under S.

But H and K are distinct words of the same actual image. A finite presentation of that image must connect them, contradiction. This proves AF2's main assertion. No increasing auxiliary alphabet or relations depending on p,q are permitted in a single finite presentation; AF1 covers every fixed finite presentation at once.

A finite complete rewriting system presenting M0 has finitely many equations (orientations forgotten) whose congruence is its equality, hence would be a finite presentation. A finite-alphabet trace monoid has a presentation by the finitely many declared commuting-letter pairs, hence is finitely presented; isomorphism would transfer such a presentation to M0. These are the two stated consequences, with SAME abstract monoid essential. Neither conclusion concerns a larger ambient monoid or a different encoding/counting object.
