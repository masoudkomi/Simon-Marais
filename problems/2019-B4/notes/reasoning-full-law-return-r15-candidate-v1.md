## 2. Vertex local times as edge incidence, with endpoints retained

For a word w of length l define E_w(h) to be the number of its steps
crossing edge{h,h+1} in EITHER direction. Let

    D_w(h)=E_w(h-1)+E_w(h),
    e_w(h)=1_{h=0}+1_{h=H_w(l)}.

Every nonendpoint visit in the time-indexed path has two incident steps;
the initial and final visits each have one. When the endpoints coincide
the correction has value2 at that height. For l=0 it also has value2,
while there are no steps. Thus, at EVERY h and for EVERY l>=0,

    2 #{0<=i<=l:H_w(i)=h}=D_w(h)+e_w(h).                  (2.1)

Moreover sum E_w=l, sum D_w=2l and sum e_w=2. These are finite
counting identities, not stationary or limiting occupation assumptions.

Write K(x,y)=1_{x+y in{0,1}}, and for finitely supported vectors
P,Q write K(P,Q)=sum_(x,y)P_x Q_y K(x,y). By(2.1),

    Q(X)=1/4 [K(D_u,D_v)+K(D_u,e_v)
                         +K(e_u,D_v)+K(e_u,e_v)].        (2.2)

All entries of K are0 or1. The last three terms, after division by4,
are bounded respectively by a,b,1 from the total masses just given.
Consequently

    Q(X)<=K(D_u,D_v)/4+N+1.                              (2.3)

This includes empty tapes and N0, with no probability normalization.

For one u edge with lower endpoint h and one v edge with lower endpoint
k, put r=h+k. Their four endpoint sums are r,r+1,r+1,r+2. The
number lying in{0,1} is the EXACT integer kernel

    kappa(r)=3 if r=-1 or0,
             1 if r=-2 or1,
             0 otherwise.                               (2.4)

Thus

    K(D_u,D_v)=sum_(h,k)E_u(h)E_v(k)kappa(h+k)<=3ab.      (2.5)

Equations(2.3)--(2.5) and ab<=(a+b)^2/4 prove(1.1). The
argument is valid for arbitrary binary tape pairs at the Q level, and
therefore for all actual pairs at the V level. It never treats all
height matches as reachable.

## 5. Counting all exceptional positions, bits and interval choices

The following upper count concerns arbitrary binary words, without
pretending they are actual contexts. This is allowed because it is a
counting SUPERSET; the actual normalization is applied only afterward.

Fix a tape split a+b=N, two absolute edge intervals
I={s,s+1},J={t,t+1}, and a set S of m positions among the N tape
letters, where positions mean concatenation u then v with the split
still recorded. Require the steps outside the corresponding interval
to occur exactly at S. There are at most2^m choices for those bits.

Between outside steps, each tape has maximal consecutive blocks using
only its two indicated edges. Each such block walks on three vertices.
For ANY fixed initial vertex and any block length l, its number of
allowed bit words is at most2^ceil(l/2). Indeed any pair of successive
steps has at most two choices from any of the three vertices, and a
last single step has at most two. If the starting vertex lies outside
the three vertices, there are zero choices. No free new starting height
is supplied at a block boundary: the earlier bits determine it, with
initial tape heights both0.

Across both tapes there are at most m+2 nonempty inside blocks. If
their lengths are l_i, then sum l_i=N-m and

    sum_i ceil(l_i/2)<=(N-m+m+2)/2=N/2+1.                 (5.1)

Multiplying the conditional upper bounds as bits are produced along
each tape proves that for these FIXED parameters the number of pairs
is at most2^(N/2+1+m). We count each word at most once for a fixed
chosen description; multiple interval choices are safely overcounted.
There is no history count, independent-path law, unrecorded cut,
outside exit matching, phase replacement or factorial correspondence.

For 0<rho<1/2, any interval with inside fraction at least1-rho
contains at least one used edge. Used lower edge heights lie between
-N and N-1. A two-edge interval containing one of them can therefore
be chosen with lower label in[-N-1,N+1], a deliberately loose total
of2N+3 choices. Both intervals cost at most(2N+3)^2. There are
N+1 tape splits. Choosing all m exceptional positions costs binom(N,m).
Thus EVERY actual output in the event of FL15C is included in the
following full upper count:

    2(N+1)(2N+3)^2 * 2^(N/2)
                    *sum_(0<=m<=floor(rho N))binom(N,m)2^m. (5.2)

The displayed polynomial records both absolute height intervals and
the tape split. The binomial is essential; exceptional positions are
not free. All global output coordinates and bits are retained.

For completeness, let x=rho/(1-rho)<1. For m<=rho*N, x^m>=x^(rho*N),
so the binomial theorem gives

    sum_(m<=rho*N)binom(N,m)
       <=x^(-rho*N)(1+x)^N=exp[N h(rho)].                 (5.3)

Also2^m<=2^(rho*N). Apply these inequalities in(5.2) to prove(1.6).
Divide by the ACTUAL all-length lower bound c_N>=lambda^N to prove
(1.7). A raw independent-walk probability is never equated with the
uniform DISTINCT R_N probability. No membership test or enumeration
of any growing finite language was performed.

## 8. Failed routes and rejected extensions

1. **Height matching as actual reachability: rejected.** AT's actual
   ruler-mountain example has quadratic Q but linear V. We use only
   V<=Q; sufficiency is proved solely for the displayed sharp witness.
2. **An early linear O bound: rejected.** A proposed Q<=N O-O² would
   fail even on unequal-length alternating-tape actual pairs. With
   one long alternating tape and an all-zero macro other tape, AR gives
   Q and V growing as3ab/4, while O is controlled by the shorter tape.
   Concretely for((01)^(2m),(01)^m), m>=1, actual full phase-correct
   witnesses as in Section3 give N=6m, Q=V=6m²+6m+1 and O=m+1.
   But N O-O²=5m²+4m-1, strictly smaller. No such replacement is used
   in the present kernel argument.
3. **Independent random-walk local times: rejected as a law.** Raw
   tapes are useful as a counting superset, but the actual uniform law
   does not make them independent. Our bound pays every descriptor
   and divides by c_N only at the end. No LDP is imported.
4. **Fixed-range packing first: superseded, not refuted.** From(1.4),
   outside count m<=rho*N splits at most m+2 inside blocks. Discarding
   blocks shorter than T leaves range<=2 packing of length at least
   N-Tm-2(T-1). For sufficiently small rho relative to an accepted CT
   threshold T, CT would give another actual upper tail near the ceiling.
   The final direct three-vertex count is stronger here and avoids
   depending on CT's very weak quantitative T.
5. **Extend localization to every positive density: unresolved.** At
   small density a positive kernel average need only concentrate a
   fraction of edge mass. It need not supply the almost-complete
   two-edge confinement used in(5.1)--(5.3). No missing implication is
   filled by declaring its exceptional steps free.
6. **Choose ever larger ranges to capture occupation: circular.**
   Escaping a large window costs steps, but the fixed-range theorem's
   required minimum block length also depends on that range. There is
   no established choice making the required inequalities simultaneous.
   AU's actual positive-area families warn against that unproved uniformity.
7. **Ambiguous grammar growth: not an input.** Published unweighted
   growth sensitivity has unambiguity hypotheses. Derivation-weighted
   statements do not count our distinct physical words. This argument
   does not construct an unambiguous full grammar or prove inherent
   ambiguity of the official language.
