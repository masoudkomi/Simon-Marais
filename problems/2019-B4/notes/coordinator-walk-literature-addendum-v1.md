## Exact convention and hypothesis differences

Their Definition4.1 allows ONLY one axis increment per source time:
alpha increment plus beta increment=1. Standard discrete Frechet also
allows diagonal increments. Our DG1 supplies its own special refinement
argument at the HALF-INTEGER offset and threshold1/2, so that distinction
is accounted for. It is not a generic equality of these conventions.
The source's walks start at0, while our Q starts at1/2. Our admitted
pairs imply an axis matching between H_u and -H_v of cost<=1, by
removing the half offset, but that weaker symmetric-strip event is not
asserted equivalent to the actual one-sided{0,1} strip.

Lemma4.3 uses a growing threshold d=n^(1/4-epsilon),0<epsilon<1/4,
with d>=(2/3)ln n and n sufficiently large. Lemma4.7 requires
Delta>=2log n. These hypotheses FAIL at the fixed d=1 or1/2 relevant
to exact B4 growth, so direct substitution of a constant threshold
would be invalid. Its e.g.d=n^(1/5) conclusion is a stretched-
exponential probability upper bound. Our already accepted lambda<2,
together with the PROPOSED DG4 identity, would give exponential
rarity at the fixed threshold, so this source theorem is not itself
a numerical improvement or an exact lambda evaluation. No such
consequence is claimed or used to accept DG.

The written proof's first-passage and milestone ideas remain useful.
It extracts a long ordered sequence of spatially separated milestones
in one walk, then bounds how quickly an independent walk can visit
the matching neighborhoods. Its probabilities and its chosen scale
are designed for algebraic-complexity applications. The new research
question is whether a fixed-scale renewal description of actual
compatible pairs, retaining all possible asynchronous choices without
double counting, can yield the exact exponential rate or a useful
obstruction-tail estimate. That description has NOT been derived.
A simple single-pair compatibility algorithm is insufficient.

I do not claim an adversarial verification of EVERY sentence of the
