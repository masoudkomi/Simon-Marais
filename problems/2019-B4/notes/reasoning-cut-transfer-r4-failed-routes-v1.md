## F1. Independent confined-walk counting for arbitrary fixed range

The accepted CR finite path-vector estimate is strong for range1/3.
Blindly counting two independent confined binary walks for a large
fixed range does not automatically beat the actual lambda: their
available walk growth approaches2, while lambda<2. Even exact walk
counts do not prove an entropy gap after dropping actual synchronization.
The replacement in CT1--CT2 is a reversible insertion into actual output
pairs. The original legal witness is used only to locate the two middle
words. The generic multivalued-map method is established mathematics.

## F2. A per-component polynomial boundary bill is too expensive

For m separate blocks, recording4m independent endpoints by(n+1)^(4m)
would cost exp(O(n log n/T)) when m is linear in n. This is not an
o(n) bill at fixed T and cannot be paid by the fixed marker advantage.
The exact repair within this NEW construction is two nondecreasing
endpoint arrays, with binomial costs giving exp(O(n log T/T)).
This is why the entire packing mass can be handled, not only one
long factor. Empty other blocks and repeated endpoints must remain.

## F3. Fixed-range longest pieces do not cover a high-cut event

Accepted CN already refutes every pointwise bound on actual cuts by
finitely many fixed-range longest-piece statistics. Its density may
depend on range; one might try choosing the range after fixing a
positive density epsilon. CT3 closes that particular loophole by actual
padding and local cut clusters: the SAME epsilon0=1/84 works for every
fixed range. After that range and its periods are fixed, pieces stay
bounded as m tends to infinity. Thus for all sufficiently large T its
packing and covered masses are zero, not positive density.

This does NOT disprove every probabilistic cover. In particular the
constructed flat has only polynomially many outputs at each macrodegree,
so it has negligible mass. The proof does not enumerate all periods,
show high-cut outputs are periodic, or control the entropy of a union
of flats with varying descriptions. Those are precisely missing steps.

## F4. One short rank-one separator is not a bounded-fibre injection

The actual map(X,Z)->X*(0,1)*Z can have N-2 preimages at fixed input
degrees2N and2N-2, all with the SAME output. Section7 supplies explicit
histories for every input and all endpoints. Therefore its bare
bounded-incoming-arrow assertion is false. This is not a refutation
of all separator schemes, average incoming-arrow control, or bounded
normalized counts. A larger marker or a statistical separator would
require its own new decoding theorem and an affordable entropy cost.

## F5. No unproved synchronized-component cover was inserted

It is tempting to treat positive cut density as repeated/commuting
periodic pieces, then charge a short description of each period. No
proof here shows that arbitrary full-history cut components admit that
description. Height equality is not actual prefix/suffix feasibility,
different histories are not different outputs, and a large set of cuts
is not automatically one long confined block. The new bounded-range
packing estimate addresses an actual mass statistic but does not repair
any of these missing interfaces by naming it a component theorem.
