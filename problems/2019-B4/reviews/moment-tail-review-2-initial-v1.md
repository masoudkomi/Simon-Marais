## 2. M1: arbitrary obstruction subsets, including infinite ones

Let G be the actual subset of F in the statement, and let
H=F-G coefficientwise. Since G contains all words of length
less than N, H has no coefficients below N. Word inclusion is
essential; no arbitrary formal power series is substituted for G.

The counts a_n of Avoid(G) are at least c_n and at least 1,
at most 2^n, and satisfy a_(m+n)<=a_m a_n by cutting words.
Consequently rho_G=inf_(n>=1)a_n^(1/n) exists,
1<=lambda<=rho_G<=2, and

    a_n>=rho_G^n, R_G=1/rho_G in [1/2,R].

It follows directly that A_G(R_G) diverges; convergence of a
nonnegative series at its radius is not being presumed.

I reconstructed the subset renewal bijection rather than assuming
regularity. Append one letter to an avoiding word. If it becomes
bad, there is a unique forbidden suffix in G: two different such
suffixes would violate factor-freeness. Removing it leaves an
avoiding prefix. Conversely an avoiding prefix followed by g in G
has no other forbidden occurrence before its final letter. One
cannot lie in the prefix or properly inside g; one crossing the
join would supply a forbidden suffix-prefix overlap. This is
excluded by the inherited nonoverlap property. Thus

    a_n=2a_(n-1)-sum_(j=1)^n g_j a_(n-j),
    A_G(z)=1/(1-2z+G(z))

formally. Every fixed coefficient uses only finitely many words,
even if G is infinite. There is no finite-state assumption.

For x<R_G all relevant series converge, as G<=F and x<R.
The formal identity then holds numerically. G(R_G) is finite,
because G<=F, R_G<=R and F(R) is finite. Sending x upwards
to R_G, A_G(x) tends to infinity and G(x) tends to its
finite boundary value. Therefore

    D_G(R_G)=1-2R_G+G(R_G)=0.

This also handles R_G=R and a multiple zero. Merely knowing
the reciprocal formally would not suffice without this
boundary-divergence argument; the packet includes it.

Write A=2-G'(R). The accepted finite first moment gives
0<=G'(R)<=F'(R)<=2 and

    0<=H'(R)=F'(R)-G'(R)<=A.

The derivative series for G converges uniformly on [0,R],
because its summands are nonnegative and bounded by the
summable values n g_n R^(n-1). Hence D_G is continuously
differentiable there, with endpoint derivatives understood
one-sided. This justifies the fundamental theorem of calculus
without requiring a finite second moment at R.

Since the missing terms have degree at least N,

    H(R)<=R H'(R)/N<=R A/N.

Also D_G(R)=-H(R) while D_G(R_G)=0, so

    H(R)=integral_(R_G)^R [2-G'(t)]dt
         >=(R-R_G)A,

because G' is nondecreasing. The directions of both inequalities
are correct. If A>0, cancellation yields
R_G>=R(1-1/N). If A=0, H'(R)=0. Since R>0 and
every possible missing degree is at least N>1, its nonnegative
coefficients must all vanish. Thus H=0, G=F and R_G=R.
No division by zero or strict derivative-gap assertion is used.

Using R_G/R=lambda/rho_G proves exactly

    (1-1/N)rho_G<=lambda<=rho_G,
    rho_G-lambda<=rho_G/N<=2/N.

The reciprocal conversion is in the correct direction.
For F_D, agreement through length 2D+1 gives N=2D+2,
and therefore rho_D-lambda<=1/(D+1), for every integer
D>=1. This includes the universal cap-1 language and is
a uniform error bound, not an asymptotic equality, a
strict monotonicity assertion, or a computational result
at particular caps. M1 checks in its complete stated scope.

## 3. M2: the sign criterion is used only through R

The function in M2 is

    E_G(x)=D_G(x)-(x/N)D_G'(x).

At R, D_G(R)=-H(R) and D_G'(R)=-A, so the preceding
tail inequality gives E_G(R)=-H(R)+RA/N>=0.
For 0<x<R, G is analytic and

    E_G'(x)=(1-1/N)D_G'(x)-(x/N)G''(x)<=0.

Here D_G'(x)=G'(x)-2<=0 and G''(x)>=0.
The coefficient 1-1/N is positive because N>1.
The finite first moment ensures continuity of E_G at R,
even if G''(R) is infinite. Continuity at 0 is immediate.
Consequently E_G(t)>=E_G(R)>=0 for 0<=t<=R.

If a positive trial r has a defined negative value E_G(r)
in the known convergence domain, assuming r<=R would
contradict that inequality. Hence r>R and lambda>1/r.
No monotonicity statement beyond R is used. This is
important because adding the infinite tail introduces
negative coefficients in E_G and a global convexity
shortcut would not be justified. A finite simple root
of any denominator is likewise unnecessary.

The endpoint r=R is excluded by strict negativity as well.
For the actual rational function used in M3, the loop
denominator is strictly positive at r, so both G and G'
are finite there. There is no hidden boundary-derivative
ambiguity in that application. M2 checks.
