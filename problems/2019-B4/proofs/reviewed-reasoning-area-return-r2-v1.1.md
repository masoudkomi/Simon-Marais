## 1. The exact phase clock and both directions of(1)

At a state `(i,j)`, the next left bit, if present, is `i mod2`, whereas
the next source bit is `(i+j) mod2`. Thus an L move is allowed EXACTLY
when j is even. In particular, once a history has consumed an odd number
of right letters, its next move must be R unless it is already at the
final vertex. No left move can intervene between the two letters of a
complete right pair. Those two consecutive source letters are opposite,
so every complete pair on the right must be01 or10.

If the right tape has odd length, its final unpaired letter must be the
LAST move of the full history: otherwise j odd would prohibit every
remaining L and there is no right letter left. At that move the entire
left tape of even length2a is consumed. Its source bit is consequently0.
This proves that every actual right tape has the unique form
`beta(epsilon)0^delta`. It does not infer reachability merely from
balanced bit counts.

At a boundary between complete right pairs, j is even and an arbitrary
run of left moves is legal. Let the parity of i be the left clock.
Writing the next right pair beta(epsilon_l) requires this clock to equal
epsilon_l; writing that pair leaves the clock unchanged. Start and end
clocks are both0. The minimum number of L moves needed to follow

    0, epsilon_1,...,epsilon_b,0

is its total variation, treating its binary entries as integers:

    sum_(l=0)^b |epsilon_(l+1)-epsilon_l|=2*r(epsilon),       (9)

where epsilon_0=epsilon_(b+1)=0. Each1-run has exactly one entry and one
exit, including beginning/end1-runs. Every clock change consumes at
least one L; every unchanged transition can consume zero. This proves
necessity of a>=r.

Conversely, implement each change by exactly one L, each unchanged
transition by none, and write the specified right pairs by RR at their
appropriate clocks. This consumes exactly2r left letters. Insert the
remaining2d L moves in the INITIAL even-j gap. Two consecutive L moves
return the clock to its former parity, and all needed left letters are
still the next bits of `(01)^a`. The resulting schedule produces the
displayed pair. For delta=1 append its final R0 after both complete
tapes. This proves sufficiency, including r=0 and b=0. The construction
is an existence proof; its number of schedules is never used as T_m.

## 2. Full globally viable vertices and proof of(2)

First take delta=0. A vertex with even right coordinate is `(i,2k)`,

    i>=A_k(p),       2a-i>=B_k(p),       i mod2=p.           (11)

schedule above. A_k(p) and B_k(p) both have parity p; hence the unused

Insert p into the adjacent binary entries epsilon_k,epsilon_(k+1) in

    A_k(p)+B_k(p)=2r+Delta_k(p),

    d+1-Delta_k(p)/2.                                      (13)

    (b+1)(2d+1)+2r.                                        (14)

    b(d+1).                                               (15)

## 5. Actual phase-preserving balanced blocks

For AR4, discard a possible visit at left prefix0. Of the remaining
K-1 or more visits to h, select the more common incoming bit beta,
giving q>=ceil((K-1)/2) selected visits. Immediately after such an L
move, the source time has parity e=1-beta. At that full-history vertex

    H_left=h,       H_right=e-h.

The latter identity follows because the source prefix has total height
e, independent of its L/R distribution. Thus consecutive selected
vertices have the SAME pair of tape heights and the SAME source phase.
Their intervening tape words are balanced individually, so each has
even length. The left length is positive, indeed at least2; the right
length can be zero. The actual history interval witnesses the block,
with common complementation on both tapes if its start phase is1.

There are q-1 such edge-disjoint intervals and their lengths sum to at
most N. At most N/(L+1) intervals have integer length>L. Hence at least

    q-1-N/(L+1)>=gamma*N/4-2

have length<=L when K>=gamma*N and L>=4/gamma. A negative lower bound
means no assertion of existence. The same construction works on the
right tape after swapping sides.
