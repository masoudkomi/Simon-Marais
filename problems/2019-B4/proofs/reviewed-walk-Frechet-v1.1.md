## Definitions and exact proposed results

Use the accepted ACTUAL chronological context relation R: a pair of
binary words(u,v) belongs to R if their ordered shuffle can be the
alternating source0101..., starting at0, of length |u|+|v|. The tapes
retain chronological order; the physical left tape is reversed only
when converting back to the official unmarked string. The canonical
count c_n counts EACH DISTINCT context pair once, with c0=1, and its
accepted root growth is lambda. No number of couplings/histories is
substituted for this count.

For a binary word w of length a define its integer unit walk

 H_w(0)=0,
 H_w(i+1)-H_w(i)=+1 if w_(i+1)=0, and -1 if w_(i+1)=1.

For(u,v) put P_i=H_u(i),0<=i<=a, and Q_j=1/2-H_v(j),0<=j<=b.
Regard these both as indexed vertex sequences and as their linear
polygonal interpolations. An empty tape has one vertex and a constant
curve, covered explicitly below.

A discrete coupling is an increasing path in the index rectangle from
(0,0) to(a,b), with increments(1,0),(0,1),(1,1); its cost is the largest
|P_i-Q_j| at its visited vertices. Let d_dF be the minimum cost.
The continuous distance d_F is the minimum cost of a continuous
coordinatewise nondecreasing path through the parameter rectangle,
starting/ending at its opposite corners. Equivalently it uses two
continuous nondecreasing surjective reparameterizations. Waiting is
allowed; backtracking or jumping over a vertex is not. These are the
CLASSICAL discrete/continuous Frechet definitions, attributed below.

**DG1.** For EVERY pair of finite binary words, including empty words,

 (u,v) in R iff d_dF(P,Q)<=1/2.                     (DG1)

**DG2.** For these SPECIFIC staggered unit walks, including the closed
threshold and empty cases,

 d_F(P,Q)<=1/2 iff d_dF(P,Q)<=1/2.                  (DG2)

No equality of the two distances at all thresholds, no weak/backtracking
version and no such converse for arbitrary curves is claimed.

**DG3.** Let D_m count the actual context pairs with |u|=|v|=m.
Then D_m>=1, D_(m+n)>=D_m D_n, and

 lim_(m->infinity) D_m^(1/(2m))=lambda,
 lambda=sup_(m>=1) D_m^(1/(2m)).                    (DG3)

Thus the balanced tapes have the full exponential rate, not necessarily
a positive proportion or any specified polynomial fraction of contexts.

**DG4.** For two INDEPENDENT uniform simple unit random walks of m

## 1. The height strip is exactly the alternating shuffle

After consuming i letters of u and j letters of v, the total number
of zeros minus ones is H_u(i)+H_v(j). A legal alternating source has
this height0 at even time i+j and1 at odd time. Therefore every
actual history is an AXIS-step path from(0,0) to(a,b) through vertices
satisfying

 H_u(i)+H_v(j) in{0,1}
       iff |P_i-Q_j|<=1/2.                         (2)

Conversely take ANY such axis-step path. It consumes each tape in
order. Every step changes the sum by+/-1. Starting at0 and remaining
in{0,1}, it MUST change+1,-1,+1,-1,... . Its consumed letters are
therefore EXACTLY0,1,0,1,... . This proves the iff between membership
and AXIS reachability, without an endpoint-balance premise: the
endpoint is automatically the correct0 or1 according to parity.
It counts existence of at least one history, not their number.

A discrete coupling may have diagonal steps. If both endpoints of
such a step satisfy(2), the two height increments, each+/-1, must
sum to0: sums+/-2 would leave{0,1}. At initial height0 take the
positive increment first, and at initial height1 take the negative
increment first. This inserts one allowed intermediate vertex and
replaces the diagonal by two axis steps. All other vertices and tape
order are preserved. Refining every diagonal proves DG1. Conversely
an axis history itself is a permitted discrete coupling.

The sign/offset in Q is essential. Comparing H_u directly with H_v
or omitting1/2 gives a different relation. An empty tape causes no
diagonal moves, and the same axis proof applies. Both empty tapes
give distance1/2 and the admitted empty history, as required.

## 2. Why continuous and discrete reachability coincide HERE

The forward implication d_dF<=1/2 => d_F<=1/2 is elementary: interpolate
each coupled move. The difference of the two scalar affine coordinates
is affine along that parameter segment, so its absolute value is
bounded by its endpoint maximum. This is also the standard general
continuous<=discrete inequality. The converse requires extra work;
the general estimate with an additive longest-edge error is too weak.

On EVERY grid edge, P-Q changes by+/-1 and has half-integer values
at both endpoints. Its allowed set |P-Q|<=1/2 is therefore either the
WHOLE edge, a SINGLE endpoint, or EMPTY. Indeed the preimage endpoints
are integers in the edge parameter, since the target endpoints+/-1/2
and the initial half-integer differ by an integer. There is no proper
nondegenerate allowed edge interval with a nongrid endpoint.

Inside a unit grid cell the difference is d+s epsilon-t eta, with
d a half-integer and epsilon,eta in{+1,-1}. The free set is convex,
as it is the intersection of two affine halfplanes with the square.
In particular a straight segment between two free points is free.

Here is an explicit reachability induction that avoids assuming one
can round every free interior point to the upper-right vertex. That
rounding would sometimes land at a forbidden corner. Process cells
in an order respecting their left/bottom predecessors. On a cell,
write its reachable left-edge set as L and bottom-edge set as B,
using local coordinates t and s respectively. Assume each nonempty
set has minimum0 or1 and includes that minimum. This holds on the
outer axes: each allowed edge is whole or a grid endpoint, and a
monotone path along the axis cannot jump an intervening gap.

If B is nonempty, EVERY free point on the right edge is reachable
from a chosen B point, by the straight segment: both coordinates
increase weakly. If B is empty and L nonempty, a free point(1,t)
on the right edge is reachable exactly when t>=min L; choose(0,min L)
and use convexity. If both are empty no such point is reachable,
apart from a reachable shared corner which by convention belongs
to the incoming edge set. Thus the reachable right-edge set is
its free set intersected with either[0,1], [min L,1], or the empty
set. Its minimum is again0 or1, since the free-edge endpoints and
incoming minimum are0/1. The analogous formula for the top edge
exchanges L and B. These statements also prove the induction for
all outgoing edge sets; their intervals are closed.

Strengthen the induction to say EVERY reachable GRID vertex already
has an AXIS history. For a reachable output grid vertex, the preceding
formulas choose an incoming grid vertex at the minimum of L or B.
That incoming vertex has an axis history by the earlier induction.
The segment to the output either lies on one grid edge, or connects
the cell's lower-left to its upper-right corner. In the latter case,
both diagonal endpoints are free, so the DG1 diagonal refinement
supplies a free axis route via one of the other corners. On a single
edge its free convexity suffices. Hence the strengthened induction
holds. It follows that a continuous matching reaching(a,b) gives an
axis history there. This proves the converse and DG2.

This argument uses the actual reachable subsets of incoming edges,
not just their nonempty flags without heights. It includes paths
through single free vertices and equality at distance1/2. If either
tape is empty the rectangle has no cells; along its single axis every
linear segment is bounded by its endpoint maximum, so the continuous
and discrete conditions coincide directly. Both empty cases were
handled above. No generic curve-simplification theorem is needed.

## 3. Balanced contexts carry the full rate

Let M_(a,b) count admitted chronological tape pairs of lengths a,b.
Swapping the two tapes is a bijection, so M_(a,b)=M_(b,a).
For a+b=2k and c+d=2l, chronological concatenation sends

 ((u,v),(uprime,vprime)) -> (u uprime,v vprime).

The two source blocks have EVEN lengths, so the second starts at
phase0 as required. For FIXED a,b,c,d the cuts after a letters on
the first tape and b on the second recover both inputs. Thus this
map is INJECTIVE at those fixed bidegrees. It need not be injective
when the first pair's tape lengths are forgotten; no such assertion
is made. Taking a=b=m,c=d=n gives D_(m+n)>=D_m D_n. Also D_m>=1,
for the history(LR)^m gives tapes0^m and1^m.

Now c_(2k)=sum_(a=0)^(2k) M_(a,2k-a). Some a has

 M_(a,2k-a)>=c_(2k)/(2k+1).

Concatenate an arbitrary member of that bidegree with an independently
chosen member of the SWAPPED bidegree(2k-a,a). Fixed cuts still recover
both. Tape-swap symmetry therefore gives

 [c_(2k)/(2k+1)]^2 <= D_(2k) <= c_(4k).             (3)

Take1/(4k)-th powers and use the accepted all-length root limit for c.
Both outer terms tend to lambda, so D_(2k)^(1/(4k))->lambda.
For odd m=2k+1, append the two-move chronological generator(0,1)
to each balanced context of tape length2k. Its source phase is0

recovers the input, so D_(2k+1)>=D_(2k). Together with
D_(2k+1)<=c_(4k+2) this proves the same limit at odd m, hence the
first part of DG3 for ALL m.

For every fixed m, supermultiplicativity gives D_(jm)>=D_m^j.
Taking1/(2jm)-th powers and the just-proved ALL-m limit yields
lambda>=D_m^(1/(2m)). Conversely those endpoints tend to lambda,
so their supremum is lambda. This proves the exact formula in DG3
without confusing the submultiplicativity of total context counts
