## 2. Two elementary exact scheduling identities

For integers `j>=0` set

```
E_j=0^j 1^(2j) 0^j,       O_j=1^j 0^(2j+1) 1^(j+1).
```

Here `E_0` is empty and `O_0=01`. All powers are literal word powers; zero exponents mean empty words. The lengths are `4j` and `4j+2`. Both words separately have equal numbers of zeros and ones. Their height traces are respectively

```
0 -> j -> -j -> 0,       0 -> -j -> j+1 -> 0.                  (4)
```

There are the following EXPLICIT histories, always starting at phase zero:

```
alpha_j=(LR)^(j-1) LL (RL)^(2j-1) (LR)^j       (j>=1),
beta_j =(LR)^j (RL)^(2j) RR (LR)^j             (j>=0).
```

They satisfy

```
pi(alpha_j)=(E_j,O_(j-1)),       pi(beta_j)=(E_j,O_j).          (5)
```

For the first identity, the initial `j-1` pairs put `0^(j-1)` on the left and `1^(j-1)` on the right. The pair `LL` adds a left `0` and the first left `1`. The next `2j-1` pairs `RL` put `0^(2j-1)` on the right and the remaining `1^(2j-1)` on the left. The last `j` pairs put `0^j` on the left and `1^j` on the right. The outputs are exactly the displayed words. Its length is `8j-2`, which is even.

For the second identity, the initial pairs put `0^j` on the left and `1^j` on the right, the next pairs put `1^(2j)` on the left and `0^(2j)` on the right, `RR` adds the right pair `01`, and the last pairs add left `0^j` and right `1^j`. This gives `O_j=1^j0^(2j+1)1^(j+1)` on the right. The length is `8j+2`, again even. At `j=0` this is simply `beta_0=RR`, producing `(empty,01)`; it is essential below. No `alpha_0` or `O_(-1)` is used.

Every component in (5) returns BOTH tape heights to zero. All displayed history blocks have even length. Therefore any ordered concatenation of them is a genuine phase-zero schedule for the componentwise concatenated words. This verifies actual feasibility, including both sides of every later cut; it does not infer feasibility from heights alone.

## 3. The finite ruler-word identity, proved for every index

Use finite words of positive INTEGERS, distinct from the binary tapes and history letters. Define

```
T_1=(1),
T_h=(h, tail(T_(h-1)), T_(h-1))             (h>=2),            (6)
```

where `tail` deletes the first entry. The first entry of `T_(h-1)` is `h-1`. Thus `T_h` differs from the concatenation `T_(h-1)T_(h-1)` only in increasing its FIRST entry from `h-1` to `h`. Its number of entries and entry sum are

```
N_h=2^(h-1),            S_h=2^h-1.                           (7)
```

Indeed `N_1=S_1=1`; (6) gives `N_h=2N_(h-1)` and `S_h=2S_(h-1)+1`, proving (7) by induction. In particular all entries are positive, the largest is `h`, and for `h>=2` the last entry is `1`.

Let `J_h` be `T_h` with one subtracted from every entry. For `h>=2`, deleting all zero entries of `J_h` gives EXACTLY `T_(h-1)` in order:

```
erase_zero(J_h)=T_(h-1).                                      (8)
```

Here is a direct verification without a table or an infinite-word theorem. Induction in (6) gives the equivalent formula

```
T_h=(h, 1+nu_2(1), 1+nu_2(2), ..., 1+nu_2(2^(h-1)-1)),      (9)
```

where `nu_2(j)` is the ordinary exponent of 2 dividing the POSITIVE integer `j`. Splitting these indices at `2^(h-2)` proves the same recursion (6), since `nu_2(2^(h-2)+j)=nu_2(j)` for `0<j<2^(h-2)`. Thus (9) follows, with the empty trailing list when `h=1`. In `J_h` the trailing entries are `nu_2(j)`: the odd indices give zero; writing each even index as `j=2s` gives the positive list `1+nu_2(s)` for `1<=s<2^(h-2)`. Together with the first entry `h-1`, this is `T_(h-1)` by (9). This proves (8), including `h=2`, where `J_2=(1,0)` and the positive list is `(1)`.

It follows that the positive entries of `J_hJ_h` are `T_(h-1)T_(h-1)`. They have the same number and order as `T_h`, except that their FIRST entry is `h-1` instead of `h`. This exact order identity is the whole ladder mechanism.

## 4. CN1: actual period words and the two schedules


The fast schedule concatenates `alpha_(t_1),...,alpha_(t_(N_h))`. Equation (5) gives EXACT output `(u_h,v_h)`.

Therefore the output is EXACTLY `(u_h,v_h^2)`.

No right factor is omitted: zero entries are handled by actual `RR` histories. There is no cancellation, division, signed count, asymptotic scheduling or change in factor order. Using (7), the lengths are

All elementary factors return their own height to zero. The factor `E_h` occurs; no `E_j` has `j>h`. Thus the range of `u_h` is exactly `[-h,h]`. The factor `O_(h-1)` occurs; every `O_j` used has `0<=j<=h-1`. Thus the range of `v_h` is exactly `[-(h-1),h]`. This proves the exact ranges and completes CN1.

## 5. CN2: equal-degree macros and balanced linear fibres

Fix `h>=2` and abbreviate `u=u_h,v=v_h`. Write

```
c=a+b=7*2^h-8,       d=a+2b=10*2^h-12,
L=cd/4.                                                       (12)
```

All of `a,b,c,d` are positive and divisible by four. Therefore `L` is a positive even integer. Let the actual context `A` be `d/4` copies of the fast context, and `B` be `c/4` copies of the slow context. Equations (10)--(12) give

```
A=(u^(d/4),v^(d/4)),       B=(u^(c/4),v^(c/2)),
|A|=|B|=L.                                                   (13)
```

Here total context degree is the sum of its two tape lengths. These are actual phase-zero outputs, with specified even-length histories. They commute as OUTPUT PAIRS, since each tape is a power of the same fixed word. Their histories need not commute.

First, without imposing balanced tape lengths, `A^mB^m` has at least `m+1` midpoint cuts at time `Lm`: use as its prefix any `m` macroblocks containing `j` copies of `B` and `m-j` copies of `A`, for `0<=j<=m`, and complete with the remaining macroblocks. These reordered macroblocks produce the SAME total output. The left prefix exponent is

```
(d(m-j)+cj)/4=dm/4-bj/4,
```

which strictly decreases with `j`; the resulting cuts are distinct. All are witnessed by full histories. This already proves a genuine cut-NUMBER obstruction.

To make the total tape lengths exactly equal, note that `a-b=2^h>0` and `2b-a=2^(h+1)-4>0`. Define positive INTEGERS

```
p=(2b-a)c/4,              q=(a-b)d/4.                        (14)
```

The left-minus-right length of `A` is `(a-b)d/4=q`, and that of `B` is `(a-2b)c/4=-p`. Thus the actual output

```
Y_m=A^(2pm) B^(2qm)               (m>=1)                     (15)
```

has left-minus-right length `2pm*q-2qm*p=0`. Its total degree is `2L(p+q)m`, so EACH tape has length `n_m=L(p+q)m`.

For a midpoint prefix of `(p+q)m` macroblocks, let `j` be its number of copies of `B`. The full product has `2pm` copies of `A` and `2qm` copies of `B`, so the EXACT permitted interval for these constructed macro-prefixes is

```
max(0,q-p)m <= j <= min(p+q,2q)m.                             (16)
```

Every integer in this interval is realized by taking that many `B` blocks and `(p+q)m-j` `A` blocks first, then all remaining blocks. This gives a legal prefix and a legal suffix at the even time `n_m`; it is a full scheduling witness. The interval has exactly `2min(p,q)m+1` integers: for `p>=q` it is `[0,2qm]`; for `p<q` it is `[(q-p)m,(p+q)m]`.

For consecutive values of `j`, the left prefix tape length changes by

```
a*(d-c)/4=ab/4>0.                                            (17)
```

Thus all these are DIFFERENT vertices `(i,n_m-i)`, and hence different elements of the actual composition fibre, even if some selected histories have further internal ambiguities. This proves the cut bound in (3). We do not claim that (16) lists ALL possible cuts of `Y_m`; it lists enough genuinely feasible cuts.

It remains to check the confined-piece claim, uniformly in `m`. A balanced word `w` of length `s` has a periodic height extension satisfying `H_w(t+s)=H_w(t)`. Every factor of length `s` in any power of `w` traverses one complete cyclic set of prefix vertices, so its range equals the FULL range of `w`, irrespective of the starting residue or translation to zero. If that range exceeds `r`, no factor of length at least `s` can have range at most `r`. Therefore

```
L_r(w^j)<=s-1                         (j>=0)                 (18)
```

whenever the full range exceeds `r`. The `j=0` word is empty and satisfies the same bound. Both tapes in (15) are powers of `u` and `v`; their periods have ranges `2h` and `2h-1`, both greater than the FIXED `r`. Apply (18) with `s=a,b` to obtain (3). This completes CN2 for every stated domain and all `m>=1`.

## 6. CN3: the flat is injective, but its mass is small

For fixed `h`, define `Phi(i,j)=A^i B^j`, with `i,j>=0`. Since componentwise word powers commute, this is a commutative-monoid homomorphism from `N^2` to the actual even context monoid. Its tape word exponents are

```

```

No uniqueness of a history, root of a word, or converse theorem about commuting words is used.

```

```

## 7. What is disproved, and the remaining meaningful bridge

For any fixed `r`, there can be no finite-valued function of `(L_r(u),L_r(v))` alone bounding all actual midpoint cut numbers, even when both tapes have equal length. In (3) both arguments lie in a FIXED finite integer box while the cut number tends to infinity. More strongly, no uniform estimate of the form

```
k_n(X) <= e_r(n)+F_r(L_r(u),L_r(v)),       e_r(n)=o(n),        (24)
```

can hold on all balanced actual outputs if `F_r` is finite-valued: the second term is bounded on that box and the left side has positive linear density along `n_m`. The same argument defeats any finite collection of fixed-range detectors by using their maximum range. It does not defeat estimates that include additional information, nor every adaptive range growing with `n`.

These are pointwise obstructions, not a selection of the actual renewal alternative. To emphasize the still-open target, let `K_n=k_n(X)` for uniform DISTINCT `X in R_(2n)`. The accepted bounds `1<=K_n<=n+1` imply the elementary equivalence

```
E K_n=o(n)  iff  Pr(K_n>=epsilon*n)->0 for EVERY epsilon>0.   (25)
```

The forward direction is Markov's inequality. For the reverse, split the expectation at `epsilon*n` to obtain

```
E K_n/n <= epsilon+(1+1/n)Pr(K_n>=epsilon*n),
```

then take upper limits and let `epsilon` decrease to zero. This is a formulation of the missing MASS estimate, not a new proof of it. CR5/G show that proving (25) for the actual language would exclude the finite-second-moment critical alternative; bounded mean would force positive defect. Neither estimate is established here.

The concrete route left open is to encode or bound the number of DISTINCT outputs supporting positive-density cut fibres, allowing multiscale two-rate ladders such as (6)--(15). A universal pointwise implication from absence of long fixed-range pieces to sublinear cut number is false. This does not rule out using further probabilistic structure of those pieces. Bounding cut spread, counting raw histories, or assuming universal uniqueness of a signature alignment does not justify that failed pointwise step. A proposed compression into repeated/commuting pieces must prove a cover of the entire high-cut class and account for the descriptions of its periods; this checkpoint proves neither cover nor an entropy bound for that class. Formula (23) checks only the family actually constructed here.
