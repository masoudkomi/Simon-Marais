## 1. Order and invariants give eight common ancestors

The map is cooperative on all Z^2: increasing either input coordinate
cannot decrease either output coordinate. More precisely, if u<=v
coordinatewise and u!=v, the nonnegative gap in each coordinate at
the next step is its current gap plus a nonnegative floor gap, times
2 or 3. Thus the coordinate gap which was positive remains positive.
Induction shows F^k(u)!=F^k(v) for every k>=0.

Consequently distinct points in any fixed F^k fiber are pairwise
incomparable in coordinate order. If they are sorted by increasing
first coordinate, these first coordinates strictly increase and their
second coordinates strictly DECREASE. Equal first coordinates would
make the points comparable, so cannot occur.

F preserves a modulo2 and b modulo3. All points in a common F^k
fiber therefore have the same two residues as the common output.
Successive sorted points increase a by at least2 and decrease b by
at least3. Their differences a-b thus increase by at least5. If L
points have differences in an interval of width w, then

    5(L-1)<=w.

For I the width is 23-(-13)=36, hence L<=floor(36/5)+1=8. This
proves D8A, including k=0 and negative auxiliary coordinates. It does
not assert that eight is attained by an actual fiber. It does not
count comparable states of equal growth that never merge; CF's
permanent even-a twins illustrate that different situation.
