## 1. E1: constant translates give actual rational-base expansions

Fix coprime integers p>q>=2, beta=p/q, and A_p={0,...,p−1}.
Use the rational-base convention

    val(a_k...a_0)= (1/q) sum_(i=0)^k a_i beta^i.    (1.1)

This is not the usual greedy beta-expansion convention. Let L_(p/q)
be the finite canonical words representing nonnegative integers,
with no leading zeros except that0 has the empty word. An admissible
infinite expansion has every finite prefix in0^* L_(p/q), and real
evaluation(1/q)sum a_i beta^i, with indices decreasing to−infinity.
Leading-zero copies do not count as distinct expansions.

For completeness the finite facts needed here have short proofs. For
N>0 repeatedly write

    qN=pN'+a,  0<=a<p,  N'=floor(qN/p)<N.           (1.2)

It terminates at0. Reversing the remainders produces a word with value
N; each prefix represents the corresponding earlier nonnegative integer.
For a length-m word of integer value N, multiply(1.1) by q^m and
reduce modulo p. Coprimality of p,q gives its last digit a_0=qN modp.
Its prefix value is then the integer(qN−a_0)/p. Induction proves
uniqueness among words of integer value after ignoring leading zeros.
In particular,
if a finite canonical word has value N and

    qN_next=pN+d,  d in A_p, N_next a positive integer,

appending d gives the canonical word for N_next. Its last remainder in
(1.2) is exactly d, and the recovered preceding integer is N. Every
finite prefix remains admissible.

Now suppose an ORDINARY integer sequence satisfies

    qQ_(n+1)=pQ_n+h_n,  L<=h_n<=U                 (1.3)

for bounded integers L,U. Its real growth coefficient

    C=lim_n beta^(-n)Q_n
      =Q_0+sum_(n>=0) h_n/(p beta^n)               (1.4)

exists by absolute convergence. Assume C>0. The same geometric tail
bound gives Q_n=C beta^n+O_(p,q,L,U)(1), so Q_n tends to positive
infinity. Define the finite interval of integer shifts

    Cset={c in Z: L+(p−q)c>=0,
                  U+(p−q)c<=p−1}.                (1.5)

If it is empty, E1 asserts nothing. Otherwise choose N so Q_n>max Cset
for all n>=N. For each c in Cset put

    Z_n^(c)=Q_(N+n)−c,
    d_n^(c)=h_(N+n)+(p−q)c in A_p.                (1.6)

All Z_n are positive ordinary integers and
qZ_(n+1)=pZ_n+d_n. Begin with the finite canonical representation of
Q_N−c and append the entire d^(c) tail. By the preceding argument,
EVERY finite prefix is in the integer language. This is an actual
admissible expansion; an arbitrary digit series without this prefix
check would not suffice.

Place the radix point after the finite seed word. The real evaluation
of the resulting infinite word is

    Q_N−c+sum_(n>=0) d_n^(c)/(p beta^n)
      =Q_N+sum_(n>=0) h_(N+n)/(p beta^n)
      =beta^N C.                                  (1.7)

Here sum_(n>=0)(p−q)c/(p beta^n)=c, exactly. Thus all shifts give
the SAME positive real value. Their tails differ at every position by
the nonzero constant(p−q)(c−c') when c!=c', so they are genuinely
distinct, even after aligning their radix points and removing leading
zeros. This proves at least |Cset| distinct admissible expansions.

If the preferred definition permits only purely fractional words
starting at the root, pad the finitely many seed words on the left to
one common length m, then put the radix point before them. All prefixes
are still in0^* L_(p/q). All values are now the common positive
beta^(N−m)C, and the tails still differ. This is the same expansion
multiplicity after ONE common scaling, not a separate scaling for each c.

E1 uses ordinary integrality and positive real growth. It cannot be
applied just to an arbitrary Z10 controller word. Its digits range over
the FULL base-p alphabet0,...,p−1, not automatically the smaller ceiling
alphabet0,...,q−1.
