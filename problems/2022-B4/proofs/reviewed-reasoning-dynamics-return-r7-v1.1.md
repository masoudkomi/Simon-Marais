## 3. Merger geometry and diagonal parents, rederived in full

The following is the accepted Q1 geometry; it is not a new claim of
classification. Suppose distinct integer inputs have the same F image.
They cannot be coordinatewise ordered, since in that case each
coordinate gap is preserved or increased by a nonnegative floor gap.
Their differences are multiples of 2 and 3 because output differences
preserve the respective input congruences. Relabel to write them as

    (a,b), (a+2u,b-3v), u,v positive integers.

Equality of images gives

    u=floor(b/20)-floor((b-3v)/20)<=ceil(3v/20),
    v=floor((a+2u)/30)-floor(a/30)<=ceil(u/15).

Since ceil(t)<t+1, substitution gives

    u<3v/20+1<u/100+23/20,
    u<115/99<2.

Thus u=1 and then v<=ceil(1/15)=1. The pair differs by exactly
(2,-3). Conversely it has the same image exactly when

    a mod30 in{28,29}, b mod20 in{0,1,2}.         (3.1)

The upper partner has its residues in

    a mod30 in{0,1}, b mod20 in{17,18,19}.        (3.2)

All floor assertions are valid for negative auxiliary integers as
well as positive ones. Three distinct inputs cannot have the same
image: their pair differences would have to be +/- (2,-3), so two
of them would instead differ by 2*(2,-3). Hence there are at most
two inputs in every image fiber.

No diagonal input participates in such a pair. For (3.1), a diagonal

    F^(-1)(F(c,c))={(c,c)} for EVERY integer c.   (3.3)
